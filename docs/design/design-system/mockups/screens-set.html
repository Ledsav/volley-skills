The full set of screens in the v2 direction

Nine screens covering every area of the app, all with the same functions as today. Each one has a note on the UX change. Scroll down, and tell me in the terminal which ones you like and which feel wrong.

1 · Team: Roster

The seven tabs (Roster, Calendar, Matches, Stats, Plan, Physical, Settings) become one segmented control under a large title. The roster is a grouped list sorted by position, with jersey number, level meter and age group. Right-click (or long-press on the phone) gives quick actions. "Add player" moves to the toolbar.

2 · Player card

A profile header like Apple's Contacts and Health: avatar, name, number, position, level. Under it, four tiles for overall level, last match rating, jump and growth. The 8 skills are one list where each bar shows the score in its level colour, and you tap a skill to edit it in place, with no separate form. Development plan, Matches, Physical and Contact are a segmented control. Guardians sit under Contact, behind a lock icon.

3 · Team: Calendar

A month grid like Apple Calendar. Sessions are small solid pills and matches are outlined pills. Tapping a day opens a side panel (a bottom sheet on the phone) with that day's session, its parts and "Share PDF". Assigning a training becomes a sheet with search, not a dialog.

4 · Live scouting (full screen, tablet / landscape phone)

Always dark, so it's easier on the eyes and battery during a match and the court stands out. A huge glanceable score at the top, and the court as the main surface. Tapping a player opens a skill-then-grade picker right next to the finger: S R A B D E F, then = / - ! + #. Grades use red for errors, orange for poor, neutral for OK and green for good. The rally bar and timeline sit at the bottom, and Undo is on its own, away from timeouts and subs (as you asked before).

5 · Match report

Set scores across the top like a sports app, then a skills table. Each skill shows a stacked grade bar (the same six grade colours as scouting), the efficiency figure and the count. Per-player rows open on tap. Awards and Export (PDF/CSV) live in the toolbar.

6 · Trainings library + builder (sheet)

The library is a searchable list with filter chips. "New training" opens a large sheet over the library: you add parts from the exercise library on the right by tapping or dragging, reorder them by dragging, and see the total time as you go. Suggestions and AI draft are one button, not a separate flow. Dimming the page behind it shows this is a focused task.

7 · Exercise library

A gallery where every exercise card leads with its court diagram, like Apple Photos or Freeform, so coaches recognise a drill by its shape. Filters are chips. Opening one shows a detail sheet with steps and diagrams. The diagram editor stays full screen, with a floating glass tool palette.

8 · Physical testing session (phone) · 9 · Sign in

The live testing session becomes a focused full-screen flow like Apple's Stopwatch: one player and one quality at a time, a huge timer or number pad, then the next player. Sign in is a single calm screen with an email field and "Send sign-in link", then a "Check your inbox" state with motion.
