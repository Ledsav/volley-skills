# Sell the club layer DataVolley leaves open

Yes, selling this app per season to Serie B-and-up clubs makes sense, but only as the club's development and training system that sits beside DataVolley or VolleyStation. It will not win as a scouting replacement. The pro scouting tier is effectively a closed two-vendor market: Data Project's DataVolley 4 (€299 Lite / €799 Pro per licence per year) and VolleyStation, both wired into federations and league data feeds. Hudl dominates US colleges. No competitor found combines a volleyball-specific skill rubric, development plans, physical testing, a club-owned drill library with a diagram builder, session PDFs and per-team calendars under club-wide role-based access. That gap is the product. A defensible price ladder is **€390 per team-season, €990 per club-season (up to 4 teams) and €2,400 for pro/unlimited clubs** (all estimates). That places the app above content libraries and DataVolley Lite, level with Sportlyzer's club package, and well below Hudl's per-team bundles and AI video. The realistic buyer pool is about 2,500-3,000 teams across Italy, the rest of Europe, US colleges and the pro leagues. On that pool, a solo founder's fifth paid season lands at roughly **€23k (conservative), €104k (base) or €319k (optimistic)** in revenue, with 6-25% of it going to payments, infrastructure and fixed costs. The optimistic case needs a federation partnership. Two things stand between the current codebase and the first invoice: multi-club tenancy, which is designed but not built, and Italian localization. The 2026-27 season is already under way, so the first real sales window is June-September 2027.

*How to read this report: figures with an inline link come from the research notes' sources. Anything marked "estimate" or "assumption", every price tier, and the whole forecast are this report's own modelling. Currency conversions use the notes' rough 1 USD ≈ €0.90.*

## About 2,800 teams can buy, and four in five sit below the pro tier

Italy alone fields about **388 senior teams at Serie B level or above** in 2025-26. The pro tiers are 12 SuperLega, 14 men's A2 and 21 men's A3 teams ([Lega Volley](https://www.legavolley.it/2025/serie-a-credem-banca-accolte-tutte-le-47-domande-di-iscrizione/)), plus 14 women's A1 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Italian_Women's_Volleyball_League)) and 19 women's A2 ([it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A2_2025-2026_(pallavolo_femminile))). Below them sit the FIPAV-run national tiers: **112 men's Serie B, 56 women's B1 and 140 women's B2** slots ([FIPAV organici 2025/26](https://guidapratica.federvolley.it/stagione-2025-2026/campionati-indoor/campionati/gp_campionati_organici_2526.pdf)). A new 16-team women's Serie A3 starts in 2026-27 ([Volley News](https://www.volleynews.it/serie-a3-femminile-dal-2026-2027-cosa-cambia-per-i-campionati-di-b1-e-b2/)). The same FIPAV lists show Perugia, Trentino, Callipo, Modena, Imoco and Chieri placing second or youth teams in B, B1 and B2. Top clubs are therefore multi-team organisations, and the right billing unit is the club rather than the team. Allowing for shared clubs, the notes estimate **about 300-340 distinct paying entities in Italy** (estimate).

Beyond Italy, the sourced core is thinner. Poland's PlusLiga has 14 teams ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_PlusLiga)) and Tauron Liga 12 ([pl.wikipedia](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauron_Liga_(2025/2026))). Germany had **95 teams apply for 1st and 2nd Bundesliga licences** ([MTV Stuttgart](https://www.stuttgarts-schoenster-sport.de/news/2-1-bundesliga/1274-95-teams-beantragen-bundesliga-lizenz-fuer-die-saison-2025-26)). Spain fields a 12-team men's Superliga ([es.wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superliga_de_voleibol_2025-26)) and **34 teams in Superliga 2** ([RFEVB](https://x.com/RFEVB/status/1947624996978696469)). Turkey's Efeler Ligi has 14 teams ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Turkish_Men%27s_Volleyball_League)). With France, the women's leagues and the smaller countries estimated, Europe outside Italy holds **about 700-1,000 teams** in its top two or three tiers (estimate; sourced core about 200). Russia is effectively not addressable because of CEV exclusion and sanctions.

The Americas pool is larger but different in kind. **348 NCAA D1 schools sponsor women's volleyball** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NCAA_Division_I_women%27s_volleyball_programs)). Add D2, about 430 D3 programs ([ProductiveRecruit](https://productiverecruit.com/womens-volleyball/division-3-colleges)), 66 men's programs ([AVCA](https://www.avca.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/2024-Mens-DI-II-Programs-10.12.23.pdf)), NAIA and NJCAA, and the total is about 1,650 college programs (partly estimated). US pro volleyball is small at about 15-20 team buyers across MLV and LOVB Pro (MLV's team count conflicts between sources; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Volleyball_(2026))). Brazil's Superliga has 24 teams, 12 per gender ([Lance!](https://www.lance.com.br/volei/superliga-distribuira-maior-premiacao-da-historia-em-2026-27.html)).

Spending power varies a lot by tier. SuperLega clubs average about **€4.5M a year** (12 clubs, about €60M combined; [Sport&Finanza](https://www.sportefinanza.it/2024/10/28/superlega-volley-budget/)). Lower down, A2 finances are described as the most exposed part of Italy's system ([iVolley](https://www.ivolleymagazine.it/2026/07/09/pallavolo-altro-quanto-guadagnano-le-pallavoliste-il-paradosso-delle-dilettanti-che-superano-le-professioniste/)), and FIPAV fee rises have hit clubs "già in difficoltà" ([Volley News](https://www.volleynews.it/pallavolo-quanto-mi-costi-societa-gia-in-difficolta-ora-anche-la-stangata-della-fipav/)). That puts willingness to pay in the low hundreds of euros per team (estimate, to be confirmed in interviews). The implication shapes everything below: most of the market by count can only pay modestly, while the clubs that can pay a lot already run DataVolley and an analyst.

## Incumbents own scouting; nobody owns the club's development record

The competitive field divides into four camps, and none of them covers the app's full scope.

**Pro scouting is DataVolley's, with VolleyStation as the challenger.** Data Project claims about 98% of national teams at the 2018 and 2019 FIVB events and 65+ leagues and federations, including the CEV ([Data Project](https://www.dataproject.com/EU/en/Volleyball)). A 2015 CEV guideline made its software mandatory for CEV matches ([CEV](https://inside.cev.eu/media/eulhmot0/dataproject-software-guidelines.pdf)). Genius Sports owns Data Project and holds an exclusive data and streaming deal with Lega Volley ([SportBusiness](https://sportbusiness.com/news/genius-serves-up-lega-volley-data-and-streaming-agreement)). An academic study calls DataVolley "the standard software" for professional .dvw scouting files in Italy and also notes that it is closed-source ([Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40537-025-01284-6)). DV4 is priced publicly: **€799/year Professional and €299/year Lite**, plus Click&Scout bench tablets **from €49/year per device** ([DV4](https://www.dataproject.com/Products/EN/en/Volleyball/DataVolley4); [Click&Scout](https://www.dataproject.com/Products/EU/en/Pallavolo/ClickAndScout)). VolleyStation is quote-only. It is official data partner for Volleyball World/FIVB events ([Volleyball World](https://en.volleyballworld.com/news/volleyball-world-and-fivb-join-forces-with-volleystation-to-revolutionize-volley)) and powers Poland's PLS. Polish press reports that it signed **about 100 US university agreements within 1.5-2 months** after Stanford became a client. It also partners with LOVB and works with MLV clubs ([xyz.pl, Feb 2026](https://xyz.pl/sztuczna-inteligencja-w-siatkowce-bank-rozbija-polska-firma-volleystation/)). Its net sales grew 60.5% in 2024 ([EMIS](https://www.emis.com/php/company-profile/PL/Volleystation_Sp_z_oo_en_4157994.html)).

**US platforms are Hudl's, which keeps consolidating.** Hudl bought VolleyMetrics in 2017 ([Hudl](https://hudl.com/blog/hudl-acquires-volleymetrics-strengthens-solutions-for-volleyball-at-every-level)), Krossover in 2019 and Balltime in February 2025. At the Balltime deal it claimed 12,000+ teams for Balltime and 40,000+ volleyball customers for Hudl overall ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/blog/hudl-expands-volleyball-focus-through-game-changing-acquisition-of-balltime)). Club volleyball is sold per team in multi-team packages: **Core $500, Plus $1,250, Premier $1,600 per team per year**. All tiers include athlete profiles, schedules and messaging, and none includes a practice planner ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/club/volleyball)). A search snippet of the same page showed single-team Plus at $2,000 and Premier at $2,500. VolleyMetrics is quote-only, and a competitor estimates it at $500-2,000 per team per year ([Varsity Score](https://www.varsityscoreapp.com/compare/hudl-volleymetrics)).

**AI video and cheap tap apps bracket the price range.** SmashVision charges **€20 per uploaded match, €40 per live game, €3,000 per season for Pro and €10,000 for Elite**. Manual tap apps run "from free to about $100 a season", for example iStatVball at $39.99 per team-season with no auto-renewal ([SmashVision, Sep 2026](https://smashvision.ai/blog/how-much-does-volleyball-stats-software-cost)).

**Training and development tools are cheap content or generic platforms.** GMS+ sells drills with diagrams at **$119 per coach per year, falling to $69 per coach for 11+ coaches** ([GMS+](https://www.goldmedalsquared.com/gms-plus)). Art of Coaching Volleyball charges $129.99 a year, or $399.99 for 5 coaches ([AOC](https://www.theartofcoachingvolleyball.com/group-membership-pricing/)). Sportlyzer shows a **€1,000/year full club package** with player evaluation and testing ([Sportlyzer](https://www.sportlyzer.com/pricing/)). XPS Network has a volleyball page but no public price ([Sideline](https://sidelinesports.com/sport/volleyball/)). At the enterprise end, Teamworks and Catapult run $11k-17k per NCAA athletics department ([SFA PO](https://www.sfasu.edu/application/procurement/contracts/B2200051.pdf)) and over $30,000 average ACV per pro team ([Catapult FY26](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/catapult-delivers-record-revenue-operating-012300534.html)). Those budgets buy monitoring, not volleyball skill development. The closest all-in-one claims come from Italy's VolleyBallSystem (season planning, training archive, match analysis; no price found; [Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/volleyballsystem.it)) and the single-coach CoAtch app (ratings, drill-library practice builder, AI practice planning; [listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/coatch-volleyball/6761017281)). Neither documents club-wide multi-team access, a custom diagram builder or minors' consent handling.

### Complement DataVolley, then become the place its data lands

The documented complaints point to the position. DV4 needs Windows 8.1+ and a USB-A dongle and does not run on ARM, so it is unavailable on Apple Silicon Macs and ARM tablets ([DV4](https://www.dataproject.com/Products/EN/en/Volleyball/DataVolley4)). One analyst's verdict is that DataVolley data "sits on the analyst's laptop", so coaches and players have to go through the analyst, and his ideal would be "DataVolley available online" ([thevolleyballanalyst](https://thevolleyballanalyst.wordpress.com/2024/01/21/hudl-vs-volleymetrics/)). Coding takes months to master ([Tacticode](https://tacticode.app/en/blog/volleyball-video-analysis-software-guide/)). Hudl's own stats show tagging errors and shallow "NCAA-style" depth (same analyst blog).

The recommended position, therefore, is "keep your scout on DataVolley; give the rest of the club everything else" (estimate-based recommendation). At pro level, the app sells development cards, physical testing, the drill library, training PDFs, calendars and the awards story to the head coach, the youth director and the S&C coach. The DataVolley analyst keeps his workflow. Below the pro tier (A3, B, B1, B2, 2. Bundesliga, Superliga 2), many teams have no paid analyst; the notes infer that an assistant or volunteer keeps stats ([coachingvb](https://coachingvb.com/comparing-ncaa-and-professional-volleyball-part-ii/) is the anecdotal basis). There, the app's tap scouting and AI text scouting can replace the DV4 Lite or Click&Scout seat outright, because it runs offline in any browser.

The highest-value item to build next is **.dvw import**, with export as a later step. Today a DV report lives on one laptop. With import, every DataVolley match becomes a shareable report and an input to player development cards in the app. That turns the incumbent from a rival into a data source, and switching cost starts to work in the app's favour. The Wrapped-style awards story appears in no competitor found. It is the feature most likely to spread through players' social feeds, and it is club marketing, not analysis.

## Charge €390 to €2,400 per club-season, priced between DataVolley and Hudl

The sourced anchors bracket the price. Below sit content libraries at about €100 per coach and DV4 Lite at €299 per licence. In the middle are DV4 Pro (€799) and Sportlyzer's club package (€1,000). Above are Hudl at €450-1,440 per team in packages, about €1,800-2,250 for a single team, and AI video at €3,000+. Enterprise AMS sits far beyond at €10k+. Volleyball buyers already accept the per-season unit: iStatVball's "Team-Season, no auto-renewal" ([iStatVball](https://istatvball.com/istatvball-subscribe/)), DV4's annual licence and SmashVision's per-season plans all use it. Hudl's playbook of multi-team bundles at up to 50% off per team ([Hudl soccer](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/club/soccer)) shows how to price a first team plus B team plus youth sector. The tiers below are the report's recommendation, and every figure in them is an estimate.

Three levers adjust these list prices, again as estimates. A **founding-customer discount of 30%, locked for two seasons**, goes to the first cohort and to the 2026-27 design partners who convert. A **10-15% federation or association member discount** follows Balltime's 10% Volleyball Alberta deal ([Volleyball Alberta](https://www.volleyballalberta.ca/balltime-x-volleyball-alberta/)) and Volleyball England's >50% off AOC ([Volleyball England](https://volleyballengland.org/get-involved/coach/coach-memberships)). A **multi-season prepay discount of about 15%** applies to two- or three-season terms. Prices should be published. Every institutional competitor (VolleyStation, VolleyMetrics, Hudl Assist, Balltime Team) is quote-only ([SmashVision](https://smashvision.ai/blog/how-much-does-volleyball-stats-software-cost)), and a solo founder cannot run a demo-driven sales motion at Hudl's scale. A 30-day full trial with an onboarding call fits better than freemium. Secondary data puts trial-to-paid at about 34% when a card or commitment is required up front, against about 3% without one and 2.4% for freemium ([DigitalApplied, citing ChartMogul](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/saas-marketing-statistics-2026-data-points-trends)).

Two counter-arguments deserve a hearing. First, the €390 Team tier could be too high for Serie B2. Those teams can buy Click&Scout at €49 and use free WhatsApp and Spond for logistics, and Spond is free and earns from payment fees ([Spond](https://help.spond.com/en/articles/25998-spond-is-free-to-download-and-use)). The answer is that B2 teams belong to clubs, so the sale is the Club tier at about €250 per team, or nothing. Second, the Pro tier could be too low for SuperLega. At that level price is not the barrier; workflow lock-in is. A cheap price signals a toy. The Pro tier should therefore be quoted with onboarding and a written DPA, and it can rise once the first marquee logo signs.

## A solo founder reaches €23k to €319k a season by year five

The forecast starts with the 2027-28 season (S1). Multi-club tenancy is unbuilt and the 2026-27 buying window has closed, so 2026-27 should be a pilot season with 5-10 free design-partner clubs. Everything in this section is an estimate. The churn ranges come from secondary SMB SaaS benchmarks: ChartMogul is cited at a 10-14% annual logo-churn median for the $1k-10k ACV band, and other sources give 15-30% ([StealthAgents](https://stealthagents.com/research/saas-churn-rate-statistics-2026); [SaaSFlywheel](https://saasflywheel.io/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks-2026)). Those are pushed up for clubs that churn on relegation, a lost sponsor or a departing coach. The adoption figures are sized against the roughly 2,500 buying entities above, and the ARPA reflects the tier mix after founding discounts. As a reality check from outside, Balltime reached about **$100k monthly revenue and 12,000+ teams** before its sale to Hudl ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/balltime-breakdown); [Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/blog/hudl-expands-volleyball-focus-through-game-changing-acquisition-of-balltime)). It did that with freemium AI video for youth clubs, a market ten times larger, and two founders. It is a ceiling, not a benchmark.

The cost side is small, and the sources support that. Firestore's free quota is 50,000 reads and 20,000 writes a day, with overage around $0.03 per 100k reads and $0.09 per 100k writes ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/firestore/pricing)). At an estimated 20-50k reads per club per active day, the app leaves the free Spark plan with only two or three active clubs. Blaze is therefore unavoidable, but it costs only a few dollars per club per season (estimate). LLM cost is trivial: gpt-4.1-mini is $0.40/$1.60 per million input/output tokens ([PricePerToken](https://pricepertoken.com/pricing-page/model/openai-gpt-4.1-mini)) and Groq's Llama 3.1 8B is $0.05/$0.08 ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/groq-pricing/)). Both work out at roughly €0.20-3 per club-season (estimate). The catch is architectural. Today the AI key is user-supplied and lives in the browser. A commercial plan that bundles AI needs a server-side proxy holding the vendor key with per-club token caps, which means Cloud Functions on Blaze. The alternative is to keep bring-your-own-key and accept the friction.

Payments are the largest variable cost. Paddle as merchant of record charges **5% + $0.50 per transaction** and handles VAT registration, OSS remittance and invoicing, with 2-3% more on currency conversion ([CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/compare/stripe-vs-paddle/); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/paddle-vs-stripe-vs-merchant-of-record-2026)). Stripe is 2.9% + $0.30 plus 0.5% for Stripe Tax, but one source estimates $5k-15k a year in accounting cost for self-handled EU and US sales tax ([Cut-the-SaaS](https://cut-the-saas.com/guides/stripe-vs-paddle)). At one invoice per club per season, the fixed per-transaction fee is negligible. A merchant of record is clearly right until roughly the base case's S3, and probably beyond. VAT is collected on top of the price and passed through, so it does not reduce revenue. Many amateur clubs are associations, though, and may lack the VAT standing for a B2B reverse charge (an inference to check with an Italian accountant). That makes the gross sticker price matter more at Serie B than the net. US universities buy through purchase orders, as Stephen F. Austin's Teamworks POs show ([SFA](https://www.sfasu.edu/application/procurement/contracts/B2000051.pdf)), so the Pro tier needs invoice-and-bank-transfer billing, not cards only.

Cash will be extremely seasonal. European budgets and staff are set in June-September before an August-October start, and the US college window runs about April-August (both inferences). Expect **70-80% of bookings in one quarter** (estimate). A solo founder therefore earns a season's revenue in about 12 weeks and must use the other 40 weeks to build and do outreach. The contribution figures exclude founder salary and income tax. In the conservative case the app is a paid side project. The base case reaches roughly one modest European salary by S4-S5. The optimistic case funds a small team, and the Balltime precedent of an undisclosed "8-figure" sale to Hudl suggests an exit to Hudl, VolleyStation or Genius Sports as upside ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/balltime-breakdown)).

## Start with Italy's multi-team clubs and sell through coach education

**Italy first, for structural reasons.** It holds the largest sourced pool, about 330 semi-pro teams plus 59 pro teams. It also has the multi-team club structure that the Club tier is built for, and the FIPAV lists document youth projects ("Progetto Giovani") that get priority in Serie B readmissions ([FIPAV](https://guidapratica.federvolley.it/stagione-2025-2026/campionati-indoor/campionati/gp_campionati_organici_2526.pdf)). The app grew out of an under-17 player-card spreadsheet. Its strongest features (skill rubric, development plans, physical testing, consent-gated minors' data) serve a club's youth sector and its B/B1/B2 teams better than a SuperLega first team. The ideal first buyer is therefore the **youth or technical director of a Serie A2-to-B club running three to eight teams**. The scoutman is not the target. **Spain second** fits that profile: a 34-team Superliga 2 below a 12-team Superliga, with no local development tool surfaced in the research. Poland's PlusLiga is VolleyStation's home ground, so Poland should come later. Turkey and Brazil are language-gated (Portuguese, Turkish) and best approached once a reference club exists.

**In the Americas, aim at NCAA D2, D3 and NAIA before D1.** D1 programs already run VolleyMetrics video exchange plus VolleyStation analytics; the University of Wisconsin uses both ([Big Ten Central](https://big10central.com/school/wisconsin/ncaa-volleyball-technology-tv-scouting/article_7f49a036-1f6e-596c-b013-6c237081f20d.html)). VolleyMetrics' exchange is a network effect a newcomer cannot replace. Smaller programs have staff, English is enough, and athletes are mostly adults, so FERPA applies but COPPA largely does not. US youth clubs, with under-13s and Hudl's US/Canada-focused club sales team ([Hudl job](https://jobs.accel.com/companies/hudl/jobs/89299592-account-executive-club)), are the worst early target.

**Choose channels that fit a one-person company.** The notes find three channels with evidence behind them. **Federation and association member deals** come first: Balltime ran them through Volleyball Alberta and Excelsior ([Excelsior](https://www.xlvball.org/2024/10/21/excelsior-empire-teams-up-with-balltime-to-bring-ai-powered-volleyball-analytics-to-more-clubs/)), and AOC through Volleyball England. **Coach education** is second: FIPAV's third-level coaching course teaches statistical recording and match analysis ([FIPAV](https://www.federvolley.it/sites/default/files/Settore%20Tecnico%20+%20Scuola%20+%20Antidoping/Rilevazione%20Statistica%20e%20Match%20%20%20%20Analysis%20-%20TERZO%20GRADO%202017%20%20-%20NO%20VALUTAZIONI.pdf)), so FIPAV's regional technical committees and coach clinics are a natural place to offer free course licences. **Community and social reach** is third, which was Balltime's main growth engine ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/balltime-breakdown)); the shareable awards story is built for that. Official league partnerships are the step-change, but they need picking with care. Lega Volley is tied to Genius Sports through an exclusive data deal, and Volleyball World/FIVB is VolleyStation's. Serie B, B1 and B2 are organised by FIPAV, not by Lega Volley, so the realistic "official partner" goal is a FIPAV or regional-committee deal covering the semi-pro and youth tiers. In this report's modelling, that one deal is what separates the base case from the optimistic one. The sequence follows: 5-10 free design partners in 2026-27, published case studies by May 2027, and a founding-price campaign in June-September 2027.

## Four risks can sink the plan before price does

**Competitors closing the gap is the most serious risk.** Hudl already bundles athlete profiles, schedules and messaging into every club tier ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/club/volleyball)). It employs more than 1,800 people ([Silicon Prairie News](https://siliconprairienews.com/2026/06/hudl-celebrates-20-years-demonstrating-global-growth-and-reach-in-sports-video-analysis/)) and buys rather than builds. Adding a practice planner is a small step for it. VolleyStation moves fastest, with AI analysis from a phone behind the court in 30-40 minutes ([xyz.pl](https://xyz.pl/sztuczna-inteligencja-w-siatkowce-bank-rozbija-polska-firma-volleystation/)). In Italy specifically, VolleyBallSystem already claims season planning, a training archive and match analysis. The defence is depth where incumbents are shallow (the volleyball skill rubric, development plans, the diagram builder, youth consent), Europe-first focus where Hudl sells least, and .dvw interoperability. Interoperability makes the app the neutral layer above whichever scouting tool a club uses, and neutrality is something none of the three incumbents can offer.

**Localization is a sales prerequisite, not a polish item.** No source quantifies localization's effect on uptake. The notes infer that Serie B staff are less likely to work in English than top-flight staff with foreign coaches, and that Italian, Polish, Turkish and Portuguese matter most for pro buyers. The app is club-agnostic in branding, but its UI, guide texts, AI drafting prompts and PDF output all need Italian before the first Italian sale. Spanish follows for the second market.

**Compliance work is real but bounded.** In the EU, the club is controller and the app is processor. Sales will require an Art. 28 DPA, a sub-processor list (Google Cloud/Firebase, the LLM provider, the payment provider) and EU-region data storage (inference from standard practice). Digital consent ages differ by country: **14 in Italy and Spain, 16 in Germany and Poland** ([Bird & Bird](https://childreninthedigitalworld.twobirds.com/home/eu)). Consent flows therefore need per-country configuration. The app already strips player names before sending text to an LLM and keeps award photos memory-only, and both should be in the sales pitch. In the US, colleges will demand FERPA "school official" contract terms: an institutional service, direct control, annual-notice criteria, and no re-disclosure ([ByteBack Law](https://www.bytebacklaw.com/2017/02/5-simple-rules-for-ferpa-contracting-compliance/)). The amended COPPA Rule has been enforced since its 22 April 2026 compliance deadline. It requires verifiable parental consent for third-party disclosure and a written retention policy ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)), which is another reason to defer US youth clubs.

**Multi-tenancy is the critical path, and it is security work.** The current deployment serves one club. The deferred design puts everything under a `clubs/{clubId}` root, and all authorization lives in Firestore Security Rules with no backend. Selling to a second club therefore means rewriting the security boundary. Every rule gets a club-scope check, rules tests must prove cross-club isolation, and the build adds per-club entitlements (team caps per tier), club-scoped superadmins distinct from the platform operator, per-club export and deletion for GDPR, and billing webhooks. Webhooks and a hosted AI proxy both push the app onto Blaze with Cloud Functions. A cross-tenant leak of minors' data would end the business, so this work deserves the remaining months of the 2026-27 season. A realistic target is a production-ready multi-club build by May 2027 (estimate). Running one Firebase project per club would be a stopgap; it has already been ruled out in the project's own design notes. Finally, a solo operator is itself a risk for clubs: match-day support falls on weekends, and a Pro buyer will ask who answers when the founder is ill. A written support SLA and an escrowed data-export guarantee cost little and remove the objection.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "can this compete with DataVolley?" to "who inside a club has no tool at all?" The answer is the youth director, the B-team coach and the S&C coach at multi-team clubs. They make up the bulk of the roughly 330 Italian semi-pro teams and their equivalents abroad, and today they work on spreadsheets and WhatsApp. That buyer is cheaper per team but buys per club, so the Club tier at about €990 is the product. Pro and NCAA D1 logos are worth more as references than as revenue. The forecast shows that price is not the lever that matters: moving from €600 to €875 ARPA adds less than moving churn from 30% to 20% or securing a single FIPAV-level partnership. The founder's scarce hours belong in retention (season-to-season data that makes leaving painful, such as multi-season development histories and .dvw-fed player cards) and in one federation relationship.

The near-term implication is a strict sequence. Ship multi-club tenancy with rules tests, Italian localization, a DPA pack and .dvw import before June 2027. Run the current season with free design partners who become published references. Publish prices when the European budgeting window opens. Missing the summer 2027 window costs a full year, because in this market a season is the only clock that counts.
