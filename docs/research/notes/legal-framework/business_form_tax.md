# Business form, liability and tax options for a solo SaaS founder (Luxembourg vs Italy, EU + US sales), as of October 2026

> Not legal or tax advice. Many points below need a check by a Luxembourg *fiduciaire* / *expert-comptable* and an Italian *commercialista* before acting. Items marked **[background, not verified this session]** come from the researcher's general knowledge and were not confirmed against a source during this research. They belong to the report as hypotheses to verify, not as facts.
> Research budget note: about 18 search/fetch calls. Several official pages (guichet.lu, agenziaentrate, inps.it) did not surface directly, so many citations are reputable secondary sources (Big-4 and law firms, Italian tax press). They are marked as secondary.

## Q1. Luxembourg: sole trader (entreprise individuelle) vs SARL-S / SARL

### Takeaway
A Luxembourg sole trader needs a business permit (which requires a real physical establishment in Luxembourg), CCSS registration (with an exemption for very small side income), and benefits from a VAT franchise raised to €50,000 from 2025, but liability is unlimited. A SARL-S (capital €1–€12,000, no notary, natural persons only) or SARL (€12,000 capital, notary) gives a corporate wrapper taxed at about 14–16% CIT plus 7% surcharge plus municipal business tax (about 23.87% combined in Luxembourg City above €200k), with a minimum net wealth tax from €535. Luxembourg only makes sense while the founder actually lives and works there.

### Cited Findings
**Business permit (autorisation d'établissement)**
- Business permits are issued for regular and lasting activities, and Luxembourg law requires a ministerial authorisation for any regulated activity — [Chambre de Commerce brochure (cc.lu)](https://cc.lu/fileadmin/user_upload/tx_ccpress/Entreprises_magazine_10-22.pdf)
- The applicant must prove a stable physical establishment in Luxembourg with day-to-day management on site. A letterbox is not accepted — [Frontaliers Grand Est (secondary)](https://frontaliers-grandest.eu/accueil/employeurs/entreprendre/luxembourg/)
- The application goes through MyGuichet.lu with a €50 chancellery fee. The decision comes within 3 months of a complete file, and silence counts as approval — [Frontaliers Grand Est (secondary)](https://frontaliers-grandest.eu/accueil/employeurs/entreprendre/luxembourg/)
- A court ruling refers to self-employed activity carried out "à titre principal ou accessoire" without a permit as an offence. This suggests that a side activity is not exempt from the permit — [Cour supérieure de justice ruling (data.public.lu)](https://download.data.public.lu/resources/cour-superieure-de-justice-chambre-10-correctionnelle/20240827-221941/20150204-ca10-43a-accessible.pdf)
- For a SARL-S, the business licence must be obtained before formation, and its number must be filed with the RCS — [LexGO](https://www.lexgo.lu/en/news-and-articles/3782-simplified-s-a-r-l); sources disagree on the order of the steps: [Damalion](https://www.damalion.com/2023/05/17/how-entrepreneurs-register-their-luxembourg-simplified-limited-liability-company-sarl-s/)

**Social security (CCSS)**
- People in self-employment, as a main or side activity, are exempt from social-security registration if their earned income does not exceed 1/3 of the annual social minimum wage. Luxembourg residents may still register voluntarily — [guichet.public.lu – affiliation indépendant](https://guichet.public.lu/en/entreprises/creation-developpement/obligations-fiscales-sociales/securite-sociale/affiliation-independant.html)
- One fiduciary puts that cut-off at about €8,284 a year, based on a minimum wage of about €24,853. That is an older figure and the minimum wage is indexed. The same source limits the exemption to sickness, accident and pension contributions — [Fiduciaire LPG (secondary)](https://fiduciaire-lpg.lu/en/publications/labour-law/combination-activities-combined-employee-status-and-self-employment)
- A person who is self-employed in one Member State and employed in Luxembourg registers for both activities in Luxembourg only, and vice versa — [guichet.public.lu](https://guichet.public.lu/en/entreprises/creation-developpement/obligations-fiscales-sociales/securite-sociale/affiliation-independant.html)
- Self-employed people pay both the employer and the employee shares, and other income is included in the contribution base — [Fiduciaire LPG (secondary)](https://fiduciaire-lpg.lu/en/publications/labour-law/self-employed-status-luxembourg)
- A sole proprietor must notify the CCSS within 8 days of starting the activity — [Deel guide (secondary)](https://www.deel.com/blog/sole-proprietorship-luxembourg/)

**VAT franchise**
- The franchise threshold rose from €35,000 to €50,000 on 1 January 2025. Exempt businesses charge no VAT and cannot deduct input VAT — [Arendt](https://www.arendt.com/news-insights/news/vat-law-changes-on-1-january-2025/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/sales-taxes-vat-gst/1490056/vat-law-changes-on-1-january-2025)
- A business that goes over €50,000 keeps the exemption for the rest of the year up to €55,000 (a 10% tolerance) and loses it the following year. There are no sector-specific thresholds — [Luxembourg SME VAT FAQ (pfi.public.lu)](https://pfi.public.lu/dam-assets/pdf/tva/sme/faq-en.pdf)
- From 2025 there is also an EU cross-border SME scheme: EU-wide turnover must stay under €100,000, and it uses a separate "EX" number — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/sales-taxes-vat-gst/1490056/vat-law-changes-on-1-january-2025); [pfi.public.lu FAQ](https://pfi.public.lu/dam-assets/pdf/tva/sme/faq-en.pdf)

**SARL-S / SARL**
- SARL-S: capital between €1 and €12,000, formed by private deed with no notary, open only to natural persons as shareholders and managers. Each year 5% of profits go to a reserve until capital plus reserve reach €12,000 — [LexGO](https://www.lexgo.lu/en/news-and-articles/3782-simplified-s-a-r-l)
- **Liability caveat (sources conflict):** LexGO states that SARL-S shareholders are "jointly and severally liable for the obligations of the S.à r.l.-S until it is converted or dissolved" — [LexGO](https://www.lexgo.lu/en/news-and-articles/3782-simplified-s-a-r-l). This contradicts the usual description of the SARL-S as a limited-liability company, and may be a misreading or refer to a narrow case. **A fiduciaire or lawyer must confirm this before relying on a SARL-S as a liability shield.**
- Registration in the RCS is mandatory. Without a notary, the founder files through lbr.lu — [cc.lu](https://cc.lu/toute-linformation/publications/download/767/43882)
- Non-residents can form one if they have fixed premises in Luxembourg, not just a postal address — [my-life.lu (secondary)](https://my-life.lu/en/?p=17027)
- Costs beyond the €1 capital include RCS publication fees and the authorisations — [Damalion](https://www.damalion.com/2023/09/02/comprehensive-guide-to-establishing-your-sarl-s-in-luxembourg/)

**Corporate taxes**
- CIT from tax year 2025: 14% on taxable income below €175,000; €24,500 plus 30% of the amount above €175,000 for €175,000–€200,000; 16% above €200,000. The 2024 rates were 15% and 17%. A 7% solidarity surtax applies to the CIT amount, giving 17.12% on the top bracket. Municipal business tax is 6.75% in Luxembourg City, for a combined rate of 23.87% in 2025 (24.94% in 2024). PwC shows no separate 2026 rate change — [PwC Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/Luxembourg/Corporate/Taxes-on-corporate-income)
- From 1 January 2025 the fixed minimum net wealth tax became a progressive scale from €535 to €4,815, based on total balance sheet — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-au/knowledge/publications/659b290b/2025-luxembourg-tax-landscape-key-updates-you-need-to-know); bracket thresholds not verified
- A practitioner site mentions an automatic €17,500 tax-free deduction for municipal business tax — [luxtoday (secondary)](https://luxtoday.lu/en/knowledge/luxembourg-tax-system-for-small-businesses)

### Inferences
- **The physical-establishment requirement links the Luxembourg options to living in Luxembourg.** Once the founder moves to Italy, a Luxembourg sole trader or SARL-S would need real premises and day-to-day management in Luxembourg. Otherwise it risks losing its permit, and for a company, being treated as tax-resident in Italy (see Q3).
- A Luxembourg sole trader is a sensible zero-cost start only while the founder lives in Luxembourg, revenue stays under the €50k VAT franchise, and liability exposure is acceptable or insured.
- For small profits a Luxembourg company pays roughly 14% × 1.07 ≈ 15% CIT plus municipal business tax. That is low compared with Italy's 24% IRES plus 3.9% IRAP, but it does not matter if the founder is moving to Italy.
- **[background, not verified this session]** A full SARL needs €12,000 share capital and a notarial deed, which usually costs low thousands of euros in notary, RCS and domiciliation fees. Annual accounts filed with the RCS plus fiduciaire bookkeeping typically cost €2–5k a year. These are estimates.
- **[background, not verified this session]** Luxembourg personal income tax is progressive up to about 42%, plus a 7–9% employment-fund surcharge, so the top marginal rate is about 45.8%. Self-employed social contributions are roughly 25% of income (pension 16% plus health and dependency).

### Gaps
- Whether software/SaaS publishing needs a specific professional qualification for the *autorisation d'établissement*, or only professional integrity (*honorabilité*). guichet.lu was not retrieved. **[background, not verified this session]** General commercial activities usually need only integrity. Some IT advisory activities may be classed differently.
- The current (2026) 1/3 social-minimum-wage figure for the CCSS exemption.
- Whether one person may hold more than one SARL-S, and the true scope of the SARL-S "joint and several liability" statement.
- The exact 2026 minimum-NWT brackets. Whether the €17,500 municipal-tax allowance applies to companies or only to individuals and partnerships.
- Employment-contract rules (exclusivity or non-compete clauses) for a side activity in Luxembourg. These depend on the employer's contract and were not researched.

## Q2. Italy: ditta individuale (regime forfettario) vs SRL / SRLS; startup innovativa; residence

### Takeaway
The forfettario regime (5% substitute tax for the first 5 years if conditions are met, then 15%, on a flat profit coefficient of about 67% of revenue for programming-type ATECO codes) is very cheap up to the €85,000 revenue cap. It is unavailable if employment income in 2025 exceeded €35,000; that threshold was extended through 2026 only and may fall back to €30,000 in 2027. INPS contributions as a *commerciante* (24.48%, minimum about €4.6k a year, 35% reduction available to forfettari) are the largest cost. An SRL/SRLS costs about €1.5–4k to set up and is taxed at 24% IRES plus 3.9% IRAP, with 26% on distributed dividends. It is mainly worth it for liability, or once revenue exceeds the €85k forfettario cap and profits are partly retained. The startup-innovativa investor tax deductions appear to have lapsed at end-2025; this is contested and needs checking.

### Cited Findings
**Forfettario 2026**
- The 2026 Budget Law (L. 199/2025, art. 1 c. 27) extends the €35,000 employment-income threshold for one year only. The check uses 2025 employment income. From 2027 the threshold should return to €30,000 unless the law changes — [informazionefiscale](https://www.informazionefiscale.it/forfettario-limite-reddito-lavoro-dipendente-pensione-35000-euro); [fisco7](https://www.fisco7.it/2026/01/legge-di-bilancio-forfettari-le-novita-del-2026/); [money.it](https://www.money.it/legge-di-bilancio-2026-le-novita-per-il-regime-forfettario)
- The revenue ceiling stays at €85,000, and the cap on staff costs at €20,000 — [money.it](https://www.money.it/legge-di-bilancio-2026-le-novita-per-il-regime-forfettario)
- The threshold check does not apply if the employment ended in the previous year — [informazionefiscale](https://www.informazionefiscale.it/forfettario-limite-reddito-lavoro-dipendente-pensione-35000-euro); QuickFisco words the cessation rule differently: [QuickFisco](https://quickfisco.it/?p=35586)
- Profitability coefficients range from 40% to 86% depending on the ATECO code. "Programmatore" is listed at 67%. Taxable income is computed net of INPS contributions paid, which are the only deductible cost — [QuickFisco coefficient table](https://quickfisco.it/?p=4450). The coefficients come from Allegato 4 of L. 190/2014 and are unchanged for 2026 — [Centrofiscale](https://centrofiscale.com/tag/coefficienti-redditivita-2026/). Centrofiscale's grouping is ambiguous: it puts web designers and engineers at 78%. **The coefficient for the exact SaaS code must be confirmed.**

**INPS**
- 2026 INPS for *artigiani/commercianti*: minimum income €18,808, IVS rate 24.48% for commercianti (25.48% above €56,224), and a fixed contribution of €4,611.64 a year including maternity. Instalments are due 18 May, 20 Aug, 16 Nov 2026 and 16 Feb 2027 — [informazionefiscale](https://www.informazionefiscale.it/contributi-inps-artigiani-commercianti-importi-aliquote-2026); [leggioggi](https://www.leggioggi.it/contributi-2026-per-artigiani-e-commercianti/)
- Forfettari can get a 35% contribution reduction on application (deadline 28 Feb) — [partitaiva.it](https://www.partitaiva.it/contributi-inps-artigiani-commercianti-2026/); Fiscomania mentions a "50% reduction" for a different case: [Fiscomania](https://fiscomania.com/contributi-inps-artigiani-e-commercianti/). Reference: INPS circular n. 14 of 9 Feb 2026.

**SRL / SRLS**
- IRES 24% and IRAP 3.9% also apply to SRLS. There is no reduced rate for SRLS. Commercial sources put the combined IRES+IRAP burden at about 27.9% (indicative). An "IRES premiale" at 20% on reinvested profits was introduced by the 2025 Budget Law, with conditions — [partitaiva.it](https://www.partitaiva.it/costi-srl-costituzione-gestione-imposte-commercialista)
- Setup costs: ordinary SRL €1,500–3,000 or €2,000–4,000 including notary and registry (sources differ). SRLS: registration tax about €200, stamp duty €155–200, chamber fees €90, notary expenses €50–200. The notary is still required for the SRLS (standard ministerial model), and there is no fully online self-incorporation — [partitaiva.it](https://www.partitaiva.it/costi-srl-costituzione-gestione-imposte-commercialista); [zunapro](https://www.zunapro.com/italy/it/blog/aprire-srl-italia-guida-completa)
- An SRL's limited liability "is not absolute": directors can be personally liable, and personal guarantees break the shield — [fiscoinvestimenti](https://fiscoinvestimenti.it/?p=127)

**Startup innovativa**
- L. 193/2024 raised the "de minimis" investor deduction from 50% to 65% for investments from 2025, up to €100k a year. It is limited to the first 3 years after registration in the special section of the Registro Imprese and requires a prior application on the MIMIT platform. The ordinary regime was a 30% IRPEF deduction (up to €1M a year) and a 30% IRES deduction, with a 3-year holding period — [ecnews](https://www.ecnews.it/fiscale/mondo-professione/osservatorio-imprese-e-professioni/start-up-innovative-la-detrazione-de-minimis-tra-aperture-e-nuove-restrizioni/); [quotidianopiu](https://quotidianopiu.it/dettaglio/11241442/agevolazioni-fiscali-e-start-up-cosa-cambia-con-il-decreto-concorrenza)
- **Conflict:** Fiscomania says the 30% IRPEF, 30% IRES and 65% de minimis deductions all expired on 31 Dec 2025 and were not extended by L. 199/2025 or the Milleproroghe decree — [Fiscomania](https://fiscomania.com/detrazioni-start-up-innovative/). Reteagevolazioni says the EU authorisation for the 30% IRPEF deduction expired on 31/12/2025 — [Reteagevolazioni](https://www.reteagevolazioni.it/start-up-innovative/)

**Residence / impatriati**
- The impatriate regime (art. 5 D.Lgs. 209/2023) covers employment and self-employment (arts and professions) income produced in Italy, up to €600k a year. **Business income (reddito d'impresa), dividends and financial income are excluded** — [Namirial focus (secondary)](https://focus.namirial.com/it/regime-impatriati-2026/). The old and new regimes coexist depending on when residence was transferred — [Fiscomania](https://fiscomania.com/reddito-dei-lavoratori-autonomi-impatriati-nel-quadro-re/). The 2026 tax decree changed the regime further — [Centrofiscale](https://centrofiscale.com/regime-impatriati-decreto-fiscale-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Worked estimate (forfettario, commerciante, coefficient 67%, not verified by a commercialista).**
  - Revenue €30k: taxable before contributions €20,100. INPS about €4.9k (about €3.2k with the 35% reduction). Substitute tax about €0.8k at 5% or €2.4k at 15%. Total about €4–7k, or 14–24% of revenue.
  - Revenue €80k: taxable €53,600. INPS about €13.1k (about €8.5k reduced). Substitute tax about €2.0k at 5% or €6.1k at 15%. Total about €10.5–19k, or 13–24% of revenue.
  - No VAT is charged and there is no IRAP. Simplified bookkeeping costs about €500–1,500 a year (estimate).
- **[background, not verified this session]** The 5% start-up rate needs all of the following: no business activity in the previous 3 years; the new activity is not a mere continuation of prior employment (relevant if the founder builds the same thing for an employer); and any business taken over had revenue under the threshold. It lasts 5 years. Above €85k revenue the regime ends the following year. Above €100k it ends immediately in the same year.
- **[background, not verified this session]** Since 1 Jan 2024, all forfettari must issue electronic invoices through SdI, including the "esterometro" data for foreign customers sent via SdI.
- **INPS fund:** selling SaaS subscriptions is normally an *impresa* (commercial) activity registered in the Registro Imprese, so it falls under *gestione commercianti*. A pure software-development professional without registry enrolment would fall under *gestione separata* instead. **[background, not verified this session]** The gestione separata rate is about 26%. The classification is a commercialista decision and drives the minimum contributions: commercianti have a fixed minimum of about €4.6k a year even at low revenue; gestione separata has none.
- If the founder keeps an employment, INPS commercianti contributions may still be due on the business income. The fixed minimum may not apply in some cases of prevailing employment; this needs a commercialista.
- **SRL vs forfettario (estimate):** below €85k revenue, the forfettario almost always wins on tax because an SRL adds fixed costs (accountant about €2.5–5k a year, chamber fees, bank) and costs about 24% + 3.9% + 26% on dividends ≈ 46% on distributed profit. Above €85k, the comparison becomes a *ditta ordinaria* (progressive IRPEF up to 43% plus regional surcharges, plus INPS about 24%) vs an SRL. An SRL usually starts to pay off once profit is roughly €60–80k or more and part of it stays in the company, or immediately if liability protection is the priority. These are rules of thumb that need modelling by a commercialista.
- The impatriate regime gives **no benefit to a ditta individuale with business income or to SRL dividends**. It would help only if the founder takes Italian employment, or salaried or professional income. It may be relevant if the founder's "employment elsewhere" becomes an Italian job.
- Startup innovativa still brings non-tax benefits often cited in practice, such as free registration fees and flexible equity and stock-option rules **[background, not verified this session]**. Its investor tax credits look uncertain for 2026, so they should not drive the decision.

### Gaps
- Official Agenzia delle Entrate confirmation of the coefficient for the ATECO 2025 code that fits SaaS: 62.10 programming, 58.29 software publishing, or 63.x hosting/platforms.
- **[background, not verified this session]** The 2026 IRPEF brackets: 23% up to €28k, a second bracket possibly cut to 33% by L. 199/2025, and 43% above €50k.
- The Italian tax-residence test after the 2024 reform (D.Lgs. 209/2023 rewrote art. 2 TUIR). **[background, not verified this session]** Residence applies if, for most of the year (183 days), the person has domicile (centre of personal and family relations), residence, or physical presence in Italy, with civil-registry registration as a rebuttable presumption. If the founder is an Italian citizen in Luxembourg, they should currently be in AIRE and must deregister and register with the comune on return. Not retrieved from an official source.
- Whether startup innovativa investor deductions were reinstated in 2026.

## Q3. Cross-border: living in Luxembourg but selling mainly in Italy (or the reverse), PE risk, where to register

### Takeaway
Where the customers are does not create a taxable presence. Where the founder lives and works does. A sole trader is taxed where they are resident. A company is at risk of being taxed where it is effectively managed. So register in the country where the founder actually lives and works, and re-domicile the business when moving to Italy.

### Cited Findings
- The Luxembourg business permit requires a physical establishment with day-to-day management in Luxembourg — [Frontaliers Grand Est](https://frontaliers-grandest.eu/accueil/employeurs/entreprendre/luxembourg/); for the SARL-S as well: [my-life.lu](https://my-life.lu/en/?p=17027)
- Social security for mixed activities across Member States is coordinated so that only one country applies — [guichet.public.lu](https://guichet.public.lu/en/entreprises/creation-developpement/obligations-fiscales-sociales/securite-sociale/affiliation-independant.html)

### Inferences
- **[background, not verified this session]** Selling SaaS online to Italian clubs from Luxembourg does not, by itself, create an Italian permanent establishment if there is no fixed place of business or dependent agent in Italy (Luxembourg–Italy treaty, Art. 5, OECD model). Regular in-person onboarding visits are not a PE either. A home office or regular working base in Italy could be.
- **[background, not verified this session]** If the founder moves to Italy but keeps a Luxembourg SARL-S managed from Italy, Italy can treat the company as Italian-resident under the place-of-effective-management rule (art. 73 TUIR, "esterovestizione"). The result is double compliance plus a Luxembourg company that cannot meet its own permit's physical-establishment rule.
- Practical sequence: (a) while resident in Luxembourg, either test revenue as a Luxembourg sole trader (if the side-activity rules allow) or wait; (b) on moving to Italy, open a partita IVA forfettario or an SRL there and close the Luxembourg business; (c) avoid the year of the move overlapping two active businesses without advice. For the year of the move, the tie-breaker rules of the Luxembourg–Italy treaty and the split-year treatment need a commercialista and a fiduciaire.

### Gaps
- No official source retrieved on the Luxembourg–Italy treaty PE article or on art. 73 TUIR. Both need professional confirmation.

## Q4. VAT on B2B SaaS (electronically supplied services), Italian ASD/SSD status, OSS, Paddle

### Takeaway
Selling to a VAT-registered business (including any ASD/SSD with a partita IVA) is B2B: taxed in the customer's country, with the customer self-assessing VAT through reverse charge when the supplier is foreign. An ASD that has only a *codice fiscale* and no commercial activity is a non-taxable customer. For a foreign EU supplier, that sale is taxed in Italy at 22% once the supplier's EU-wide B2C cross-border sales exceed €10k a year, filed through OSS. Paddle as merchant of record takes over this whole problem for the revenue it processes, because Paddle becomes the legal seller.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 7-ter c.2 DPR 633/72 treats as taxable customers businesses, artists and professionals; entities and associations under art. 4 c.4 even when acting outside commercial activity; and non-taxable entities registered for VAT. Services to taxable persons established in Italy are taxed in Italy — [ecnews](https://www.ecnews.it/fiscale/fisco-e-patrimonio/iva/status-soggetto-passivo-del-committente-nei-servizi-lestero/); [fiscomania](https://fiscomania.com/prestazioni-di-servizi-iva-estero/)
- Electronic services to non-taxable persons are taxed where the customer is established (art. 7-octies) — [fiscoetasse](https://www.fiscoetasse.com/approfondimenti/15134-territorialita-iva-di-servizi-complessi-resi-in-parte-per-via-elettronica-dallestero.html)
- To declare B2C services in another Member State through the Union OSS scheme, the supplier must be established in the EU — [EU VAT One Stop Shop portal](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en)
- Paddle acts as a reseller and is the seller on record. It calculates tax at checkout by customer location, remits it, and issues compliant invoices — [Paddle help](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/how-paddle-is-able-to-take-on-your-vat-and-tax-responsibilities); [Paddle – what is a merchant of record](https://paddle.com/support/what-is-a-merchant-of-record/)
- The buyer's purchase is legally from Paddle. Paddle's invoice names Paddle as merchant of record and binds the buyer to Paddle's terms — [Usermaven docs](https://docs.usermaven.com/articles/vat-and-invoices-12c3d458)
- MoR coverage applies only to revenue processed through Paddle and is not retroactive — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/integrations/paddle/sales-tax-and-global-vat-software-for-paddle)
- In the Luxembourg franchise, exempt businesses charge no VAT — [Arendt](https://www.arendt.com/news-insights/news/vat-law-changes-on-1-january-2025/)

### Inferences
- **ASD/SSD classification for invoicing:**
  - **SSD** (a company): always a taxable person, so B2B.
  - **ASD with a partita IVA** (commercial activity, often under L. 398/1991): a taxable person under art. 7-ter even for institutional purchases, so B2B. A foreign supplier invoices without VAT ("reverse charge") and the ASD self-assesses 22%. An Italian supplier charges 22%, or no VAT if under forfettario.
  - **ASD with only a codice fiscale** (purely institutional): B2C. **[background, not verified this session]** Under Art. 18 of Implementing Regulation 282/2011, a supplier may treat a customer as non-taxable unless the customer gives a VAT number. This supports asking every club for its partita IVA and VIES status at checkout.
- **[background, not verified this session]** Under Art. 59c of the VAT Directive, an EU supplier established in a single Member State whose B2C cross-border electronic services stay at or below €10,000 a year (EU-wide total) can apply home-country VAT. Above that, customer-country VAT applies (22% in Italy, 21% in Spain, 23% in Poland, 19% in Germany), declared through Union OSS.
- **Italian founder under forfettario:** Italian ASD customers pay no VAT (an advantage for non-VAT-registered ASDs, which cannot recover VAT). EU B2B customers are invoiced with reverse charge. The forfettario business needs VIES enrolment for intra-EU services.
- **Luxembourg founder under the €50k franchise:** a domestic exemption. Italian B2B customers self-assess. Italian non-VAT ASDs: Luxembourg franchise (no VAT) up to the €10k B2C cross-border threshold, then 22% via OSS.
- **With Paddle:** clubs contract and pay Paddle. The founder has one "customer", the Paddle entity, and receives payouts. For a forfettario founder, invoicing a non-EU company (Paddle's UK entity) for services is outside the scope of Italian VAT, reported via SdI. Paddle removes OSS, US sales tax and multi-rate invoicing. **[background, not verified this session]** Paddle's fees are about 5% + $0.50 per transaction, against Stripe's lower card fees plus Stripe Tax.
- **Contract counterparty implication:** with Paddle, the *sales* contract counterparty for the club is Paddle. Software terms, the SLA and the **GDPR data-processing agreement (Art. 28) must still name the founder's business** (sole trader in their own name, or the company), because the founder processes the minors' data. With Stripe direct, the founder's business is the seller of record for everything.

### Gaps
- No official Italian source (Agenzia delle Entrate ruling) found on an ASD *without* a partita IVA buying foreign e-services.
- How a Luxembourg franchise business switches to OSS once B2C cross-border sales exceed €10k: whether it must leave the franchise or register for a VAT number. Ask the AED or a fiduciaire.
- Paddle's exact contracting entities by region and whether Italian VAT-registered clubs receive invoices they can process through SdI. Check Paddle's buyer terms.

## Q5. US: selling SaaS to US universities from the EU

### Takeaway
A foreign seller without US presence has no US federal income tax nexus and documents this with a W-8BEN-E (company) or W-8BEN (individual). State sales tax can apply through economic nexus (commonly $100k of sales or 200 transactions per state), but only about 20 states tax SaaS, and universities are usually exempt through exemption certificates. At the forecast volumes the US risk is low, and Paddle as MoR absorbs it.

### Cited Findings
- The W-8BEN-E mainly documents foreign status, and federal withholding is usually not the real risk. State sales tax is the bigger exposure — [enty.io (secondary)](https://enty.io/blog/selling-to-us-customers-estonian-ou-w8bene-sales-tax-2026)
- Economic nexus applies to foreign businesses as to US ones, with no physical presence needed — [Anchin](https://www.anchin.com/articles/anchins-u-s-expansion-playbook-understanding-sales-tax-exposure-for-foreign-businesses/)
- As of July 2025, 24 states have a $100,000 threshold and 16 have dual thresholds of $100,000 or 200 transactions — [Anchin](https://www.anchin.com/articles/anchins-u-s-expansion-playbook-understanding-sales-tax-exposure-for-foreign-businesses/)
- About 20 states tax SaaS (for example NY, WA, TX with exemptions, PA, AZ, CT) and about 30 do not — [Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/sales-tax-on-software/); [Taxually](https://taxually.com/blog/sales-tax-on-saas-a-guide-for-businesses)
- Collection duties can be reduced with customer exemption certificates — [mjcpa](https://www.mjcpa.com/?p=11404)

### Inferences
- **[background, not verified this session]** Under the Italy–US (1999) and Luxembourg–US (1996) treaties, SaaS subscription fees are normally business profits, taxable in the US only if there is a US PE. Some university payers misclassify software fees as royalties and withhold 30% unless a W-8 claims treaty benefits, so complete Part III of the W-8BEN-E. A sole trader uses a W-8BEN, and a US TIN or foreign TIN is needed on the form.
- Universities often require vendor-onboarding paperwork (supplier forms, insurance certificates, accessibility statements, data-security questionnaires). Those requirements are where the insurance and data-protection set-up matters most.

### Gaps
- No source found on state-specific university exemption rules for SaaS, or on the treaty article numbers and software royalty rates. Verify with a US tax adviser before the first US contract.

## Q6. Liability shield (minors' data) and insurance costs

### Takeaway
A sole trader (Italian ditta individuale or Luxembourg entreprise individuelle) puts all personal assets at risk for contract claims and GDPR damages. Processing minors' data raises the stakes. A company (SRL, SARL; SARL-S only after its liability point is clarified) plus cyber insurance with professional indemnity is the standard protection. Hard 2026 premium data for small SaaS was not found. The only priced benchmark is a 2019 Italian broker scheme starting at €58 a year for €250k cover, which is clearly a floor.

### Cited Findings
- A sole trader or SARL-S caveat: LexGO says SARL-S shareholders are jointly and severally liable until conversion — [LexGO](https://www.lexgo.lu/en/news-and-articles/3782-simplified-s-a-r-l) (needs verification)
- An SRL's limited liability is not absolute: directors' liability and personal guarantees are exceptions — [fiscoinvestimenti](https://fiscoinvestimenti.it/?p=127)
- Price benchmark: a 2019 Marsh scheme for professionals and small firms with turnover up to €100k quotes premiums from €58 a year, limits €250k–€3M, deductible from €500 — [CNPI / Marsh 2019](https://cnpi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/CYBER-RISK-INFORMATIVA-E-TARIFFA-2019.pdf)
- Cyber policies typically combine liability (third-party financial loss from privacy or data breaches), first-party damage and assistance — [Allianz DIP](https://www.allianz.it/content/dam/onemarketing/azit/allianz-it/le-soluzioni-per-te/impresa/mid-corporate/20190928-618-DIA-MGEN-SI.pdf)
- Some ICT schemes explicitly exclude software activities, so check policy scope — [Comufficio convention](https://comufficio.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/NI_21_Polizza_Cyber_Risk.pdf)
- Professional indemnity wording covers financial loss from errors, negligence or omissions on a claims-made basis — [Lepida scheme](https://www.lepida.net/sites/default/files/2022-11/Scheda%20Polizza%20RC%20professionale%20%28SPID%29_RC%20Cyber.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[background, not verified this session]** GDPR Art. 82 gives data subjects (here minors and their guardians) a right to compensation from controllers *and processors*. Contractual liability caps in the club terms and DPA (for example, capped at 12 months' fees) are the first line of defence. Insurance is the second, and the company form is the third.
- Realistic estimate, to verify with brokers: combined cyber + E&O/RC professionale for a micro-SaaS with under €100k revenue and €250–500k limits is likely in the low hundreds to about €1,500 a year in Italy or Luxembourg. US university customers may ask for $1M+ limits, which would cost more.
- **Recommendation logic:** before taking on paying clubs at scale or any US university contract, have (i) a company or at least robust contract caps, (ii) cyber + PI insurance, and (iii) a DPA template naming the legal entity.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–26) premium quotes for SaaS handling minors' data in Italy or Luxembourg. Get them from brokers (for example Hiscox, AXA, Allianz, digital brokers) using the actual revenue and data volume.

## Q7. Practical thresholds: when to switch form

### Takeaway
All thresholds below are estimates synthesised from the cited rates. They are not sourced recommendations.

### Cited Findings
- Forfettario cap €85k; €35k employment-income limit for 2026 — [money.it](https://www.money.it/legge-di-bilancio-2026-le-novita-per-il-regime-forfettario)
- IRES 24% + IRAP 3.9%; SRL setup €1.5–4k — [partitaiva.it](https://www.partitaiva.it/costi-srl-costituzione-gestione-imposte-commercialista)
- Luxembourg VAT franchise €50k; Luxembourg CIT 14%/16% + 7% surcharge + municipal business tax — [Arendt](https://www.arendt.com/news-insights/news/vat-law-changes-on-1-january-2025/); [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/Luxembourg/Corporate/Taxes-on-corporate-income)
- INPS commercianti fixed minimum €4,611.64 — [informazionefiscale](https://www.informazionefiscale.it/contributi-inps-artigiani-commercianti-importi-aliquote-2026)

### Inferences (estimates)
1. **Revenue €0–10k a season, founder still in Luxembourg:** consider a Luxembourg sole trader only if (a) the employment contract allows it, (b) the business permit with a physical establishment can be obtained, and (c) income stays under the CCSS 1/3-minimum-wage exemption. Otherwise wait and launch in Italy. Use Paddle to avoid VAT work. Get contract caps and a DPA from day one.
2. **€5–25k, resident in Italy:** a ditta individuale in forfettario (5% if start-up conditions are met). Check the INPS fund classification, because the €4.6k commercianti minimum weighs heavily at about €10k revenue. Clubs pay no VAT unless Paddle charges it. Buy cyber + PI insurance.
3. **€50–85k:** forfettario is still usually best on tax. If liability or US contracts matter, consider an SRL earlier, accepting about €3–6k a year of extra cost.
4. **Above €85k (realistic at €100–300k a season):** the forfettario is lost. Move to an SRL (IRES 24% + IRAP, owner paid through director fees or dividends) or a ditta ordinaria. With profit of €80k+ and partial reinvestment, an SRL is generally better and also solves liability. Have a commercialista model the director-fee and dividend mix.
5. **Startup innovativa registration** only if raising outside money. The investor deductions appear lapsed for 2026.

### Gaps
- No authoritative break-even study found. All thresholds above need a commercialista model using real figures (employment income, family situation, region and comune surcharges).
