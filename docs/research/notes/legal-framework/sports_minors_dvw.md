# Sports-specific, minors-specific and DataVolley (.dvw) legal constraints for EU club software (Italy first), as of October 2026

Source-quality key used below: **[LAW]** binding statute/regulation or CJEU judgment; **[FED]** federation/CONI rule (binding on affiliated clubs, not on vendors); **[DPA]** regulator guidance; **[COMM]** law-firm/press/club commentary; **[BK]** background knowledge from the researcher, NOT verified by a fetch in this session. Treat [BK] items as leads to check against the primary text. 19 searches/fetches were run; several primary texts (FIPAV regulation, CONI Principi text, Data Project EULA) could not be retrieved.

## 1. Italy's sport reform (D.Lgs. 36/2021, D.Lgs. 39/2021) and safeguarding duties for ASD/SSD (CONI Principi 2023, FIPAV, "Responsabile contro abusi")

### Takeaway
Art. 16 of D.Lgs. 39/2021 plus CONI Delibera 255/2023 make every ASD/SSD (including volleyball clubs affiliated to FIPAV) adopt an organisational/control model and a code of conduct protecting minors, and appoint a safeguarding officer ("Responsabile contro abusi, violenze e discriminazioni"). The deadlines (models by 31 Aug 2024, officer by 31 Dec 2024 after extension) have passed, so in 2026 every target club should already be compliant. These are obligations on the club, not on a software vendor. Still, club codes typically restrict how images and videos of minors are used, and that falls directly on award stories and photos.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] D.Lgs. 39/2021 art. 16 requires the drafting of organisational and control models for sporting activity, and of codes of conduct protecting minors. Federations, disciplines and promotion bodies issue guidelines valid for four years, adapted to their associations and clubs. — [CSAIn Linee guida MOG](https://www.csain.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/LINEE-GUIDA-PER-LA-PREDISPOSIZ.-DEI-MOG.pdf); [ecnews.it commentary](https://www.ecnews.it/fiscale/?p=28072)
- [FED] CONI Delibera n. 255 of 25 July 2023 set the content of the guidelines and created the Safeguarding Officer role. The Osservatorio permanente then issued the "Principi fondamentali" with the minimum content of models and codes. — [CSAIn Linee guida](https://www.csain.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/LINEE-GUIDA-PER-LA-PREDISPOSIZ.-DEI-MOG.pdf); the Government Sport Department publishes a template model and course material: [fac-simile modello organizzativo](https://www.sport.governo.it/media/xtxnhxgu/fac-simile_modello-organizzativo-e-di-controllo.docx), [corso responsabili safeguarding](https://www.sport.governo.it/media/acslouju/corso-responsabili-safeguarding.pdf)
- [COMM/club doc] Deadlines: the organisational model and codes of conduct were due by 31 Aug 2024, and the safeguarding officer had to be appointed by 31 Dec 2024 (already an extended date). No later extension was found. — [club safeguarding info doc (supersite)](https://files.supersite.aruba.it/media/38378_0b59dd3a0ffbad6ee308a1080210071af866b8f6.pdf); [QuiFinanza](https://quifinanza.it/sport-economy/sfs-sport-child-safeguarding/823138/amp/) also gives 31 August
- [FED] FIPAV FAQ on the officer: chosen for proven morality and competence, must be a FIPAV tesserato, and must have no convictions for listed offences (including sexual offences against minors). The role needs autonomy and independence, so in principle a coach or the club president should not hold it. The FIPAV officer may be the same person as the CONI-model officer. — [FIPAV FAQ SafeGuarding (search snippet; PDF returned 404 on direct fetch)](https://www.federvolley.it/sites/default/files/documenti/FAQ%20SafeGuarding-.pdf)
- [FED] FIPAV has a "Regolamento per la prevenzione e il contrasto ad abusi, violenze e discriminazione sui tesserati" (cited in a FIPAV Tribunale Federale decision of March 2026, so it is in force), plus an earlier annex to its Code of Ethics, "Norme comportamentali in materia di prevenzione e repressione degli abusi sui minori" (19 May 2022). — [FIPAV Codice Etico annex](https://www.federvolley.it/sites/default/files/Allegato%20al%20Codice%20Etico%20-%20norme%20comportamentali%20prevenzione%20e%20repressio....pdf); [FIPAV TF CU n.109](https://www.federvolley.it/sites/default/files/comunicati/file/TF%20-%20CU%20n%20109.pdf)
- [COMM/club code] Club codes of conduct say members must not use, reproduce or distribute images or videos of minor members except for educational purposes, and then only with parental or guardian authorisation. They also call for privacy in locker rooms and for avoiding one-to-one situations. — [PGS codice etico e di condotta](https://files.supersite.aruba.it/media/400_8bb59cd018666782945342d0c4cc818b5b52f4fa.pdf/o/codice%20etico%20e%20di%20condotta_pgs.pdf); [example club codice di condotta](https://files.supersite.aruba.it/media/35539_4c800f4d76703e2d1c55c0a7f6f4fea7a0080ffa.pdf/o/codice%20condotta.pdf); [Cuneo Volley MOG](https://cuneovolley.it/storage/pdf/Cuneo%20Volley-Modello%20Organizzativo%20e%20Codice%20Etico%20di%20condotta.pdf)
- [FED] FIGC's guidelines are a comparable sport-federation safeguarding template (useful for the structure of image and communication rules). — [FIGC linee guida safeguarding](https://figc.it/media/210673/87-linee-guida-figc-per-le-politiche-di-safeguarding.pdf)

### Inferences
- No source puts a duty on software vendors. The club is the controller and bears the safeguarding duties. The vendor (as processor) helps by making the club's model enforceable. Features that map onto typical MOG/code rules:
  - Role-based access: coaches see only their team, and guardians/viewers see only their own child. The app's per-team `adminEmails`/`viewerEmails` model already fits.
  - An image-consent flag per player, recorded with who consented, when, and for what scope. Award stories and photo features are blocked unless the flag is set.
  - No direct coach-to-minor messaging channel (codes discourage one-to-one contact), or, if one exists, guardians are copied in.
  - An audit/access log the safeguarding officer can review.
  - Optionally, a field showing the club's safeguarding officer contact (codes require the officer to be published).
- Because the officer may not be a coach, a "safeguarding officer" read-only role (sees consent records and audit trail, not necessarily skills) may be a selling point. This is design speculation, not a legal requirement.

### Gaps
- The full text of the CONI "Principi fondamentali" and of the FIPAV safeguarding regulation could not be retrieved, so their exact wording on photos, video, social media and data retention is unconfirmed.
- Whether FIPAV or CONI updated the guidelines in 2025-2026 (they are four-year guidelines from 2023) was not found.
- D.Lgs. 36/2021 provisions (e.g. on the status of minors' sports work) were not researched in depth; no data or image clause specific to software was found.

## 2. Italian Garante guidance on minors' photos/data; social sharing; digital age of consent 14 (art. 2-quinquies Codice privacy)

### Takeaway
The Garante's line (from its school guidance, which by analogy also fits sports clubs) is that parents may take photos for personal or family use, but **publishing minors' images online or on social media generally requires informed consent from those with parental responsibility**, and the consent can be withdrawn. Italy set the age of consent for information society services at 14. That age covers consent to online services only, not the publication of images or the processing of a club's data.

### Cited Findings
- [DPA via press] The Garante says photos and videos taken by parents at school plays, trips and performances do not breach privacy when kept for personal, family or friends use. Publishing them on the internet or social networks generally requires the informed consent of the holders of parental responsibility and of other people recognisable in the images. This comes from the vademecum "La scuola a prova di privacy" (2025 update per the press). — [OrizzonteScuola](https://www.orizzontescuola.it/recite-e-saggi-scolastici-foto-e-video-violano-la-privacy-le-indicazioni-del-garante/); [Tecnica della Scuola](https://www.tecnicadellascuola.it/recite-di-fine-anno-si-possono-scattare-foto-e-video-ma-per-i-social-serve-il-consenso-le-ultime-indicazioni-del-garante)
- [COMM] Commentary says consent should come from both parents, be free and informed, and be revocable at any time. Organisers (campus, oratori, educational centres) that publish activity images for promotion must inform parents first and collect specific consent. — [OrizzonteScuola (campus/oratori)](https://www.orizzontescuola.it/genitori-che-scattano-foto-durante-eventi-nei-campus-oratori-saggi-gite-estive-ricordiamo-le-regole-di-pubblicazione-nei-social-occhio-alla-privacy/); [OrizzonteScuola checklist](https://www.orizzontescuola.it/scuole-che-pubblicato-foto-video-e-audio-di-minori-non-basta-il-consenso-dei-genitori-una-proposta-di-check-list-privacy-per-essere-sicuri/); [Agenda Digitale](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/scuola-digitale/foto-e-video-dei-minori-a-scuola-come-pubblicarli-nel-rispetto-della-privacy/)
- [COMM] Italian case law reported in commentary: posting a child's school-play photo on Facebook without consent was unlawful. — [StudioCataldi](https://www.studiocataldi.it/articoli/24141-figli-illecito-pubblicare-la-foto-della-recita-su-fb.asp)
- [BK] Art. 2-quinquies of D.Lgs. 196/2003 (inserted by D.Lgs. 101/2018) sets 14 as the age at which a minor can consent to information society services offered directly to them. Under 14, consent must come from the holder of parental responsibility, and information addressed to minors must be in clear, plain language. Separately, art. 10 of the Civil Code and arts. 96-97 of Law 633/1941 (copyright law) protect a person's image. — primary text: [Normattiva, D.Lgs. 196/2003](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2003-06-30;196) (not fetched this session; verify)

### Inferences
- The Wrapped-style award story is the riskiest feature. On-device processing with no storage helps a lot, because the vendor never holds the image. But once a coach **shares** the story (social media, WhatsApp groups), the club is publishing a minor's image. The app should therefore:
  - gate the photo step on a recorded image-publication consent per player (scope "publication on club social channels"), ideally showing whether both parents consented;
  - add a "no photo" fallback (avatar or jersey number) for players without consent;
  - avoid putting full names together with the image by default (commentary advises avoiding first and last names);
  - warn the coach at share time that the story contains minors' images.
- Art. 2-quinquies (14) matters only if the minor is the user who consents to the app itself (e.g. player self-accounts). For club processing, the lawful basis is the club's (membership contract, legitimate interest, or consent for images). The 14 threshold should not be mistaken for an image-consent threshold. Italian practice, per the commentary above, asks parents for image consent for all under-18s.

### Gaps
- No Garante decision or guidance specific to **sports clubs** or sports competitions was found. The school vademecum is the closest analogue.
- The original Garante vademecum text was not fetched (only press summaries).

## 3. Other target countries: Spain, Germany, Poland

### Takeaway
Spain (LOPIVI) and Poland ("Ustawa Kamilka") impose **statutory** child-protection duties directly on sports entities: a protection delegate, protocols, and checks of criminal records for sexual offences. Germany relies on youth-welfare agreements (§72a SGB VIII) rather than a direct statutory duty on every club. Digital age of consent: Spain 14 (LOPDGDD art. 7), Germany and Poland 16 (the GDPR default, no derogation). [BK for the ages.]

### Cited Findings
- [LAW via COMM] Spain, Ley Orgánica 8/2021 (LOPIVI), Chapter IX, arts. 47-48: sports and leisure entities must create safe environments. Art. 48.1.c requires a **delegado/a de protección** to whom children can turn, who spreads protocols and reports violence. A regional ombudsman held that a protocol not clearly identifying the delegate was insufficient. — [FENAUTO protocolo](https://www.fenauto.com/wp-content/uploads/Protocolo-delegado-proteccion-del-menor-FENAUTO_R2.pdf); [Procurador del Común resolution](https://procuradordelcomun.org/archivos/resoluciones/1_1785310998.pdf); [Educo guide on the delegate in sports entities](https://admin.educo.org/getmedia/d0c60eb1-d9f7-4e7e-92c8-f2f4de58f241/Figura-de-Delegado-o-Delegada-de-Proteccion-en-entidades-deportivas.pdf); [Liceo: LOPIVI and sports entities](https://liceo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/LOPIVI-como-afecta-a-una-entidad-deportiva.pdf)
- [FED/COMM] Spanish sports protocols require staff to hold the **certificado negativo de delitos de naturaleza sexual**. The Generalitat Valenciana requires each sports entity to have a trained delegate. — [Federación Navarra de Pádel protocol](https://federacionnavarradepadel.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/PROTOCOLO-FRENTE-A-LA-VIOLENCIA-FEDERACION-NAVARRA-DE-PADEL.pdf); [Valencia Plaza](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/generalitat-crea-protocolo-prevencion-actuacion-frente-violencia-contra-menores-deporte)
- [LAW via gov] Poland: the "Ustawa Kamilka" amended the 2016 act on counteracting sexual crime and protecting minors (arts. 22b-22c). From 15 Feb 2024, organisations including **sports clubs and organisers of sports activities for minors** must adopt "standardy ochrony małoletnich" (child-protection standards), with a 6-month transition to **15 Aug 2024**. Non-compliance is fined. FDDS publishes recommendations for sports clubs and competition organisers. — [gov.pl Ministry of Justice](https://www.gov.pl/web/sprawiedliwosc/ustawa-kamilka--standardy-ochrony-maloletnich); [FDDS](https://fdds.pl/o-fundacji/co-nowego-w-fundacji/15-lutego-zaczynaja-obowiazywac-standardy-ochrony-maloletnich-co-to-oznacza-dla-organizacji-i-firm-pracujacych-z-dziecmi.html); [Rzeczpospolita](https://pro.rp.pl/prawo-dla-profesjonalistow/art40961091-nowe-obowiazki-szkol-osrodkow-sportowych-a-nawet-hoteli-cel-ochrona-dzieci); [Bezprawnik](https://bezprawnik.pl/standardy-ochrony-maloletnich/); [club recommendations for competition organisers](https://gorniczyklubsportowyrozbark.pl/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Rekomendacje-dla-organizatora-rozgrywek.pdf)
- [BK] Polish standards (art. 22c) must include rules on **protecting minors' personal data and images** and on the safe use of the internet within the organisation. Verify against the act text and the gov.pl attachment: [gov.pl attachment](https://www.gov.pl/attachment/c1574583-56dd-4ccf-989f-2a91ec00cb89) (not fetched).
- [LAW via COMM] Germany: §72a SGB VIII addresses the Jugendamt, which should reach agreements with independent providers (including sports clubs) so that people convicted of listed offences have no child contact. A club is **not legally obliged** to inspect extended criminal records (erweitertes Führungszeugnis) unless it signed a §72a agreement, which then binds it. Many clubs adopt a Kinderschutzkonzept voluntarily or under their Landessportbund. — [Sportjugend Hessen Merkblatt](https://sportjugend-hessen.de/fileadmin/media/gesellschaft/kindeswohl/Kindeswohl-Merkblatt_erw._Fuehrungszeugnis.pdf); [Hagen/Deutscher Verein doc](https://allris.hagen.de/public/wicket/resource/org.apache.wicket.Application/doc305068.pdf); [SSB Halle Musterkonzept 2025](https://sportinhalle.de/files/p_file/2026-01/2025-11-21_ssb_halle_musterkonzept_kinderschutz_im_sportverein.pdf); [SC Potsdam (volleyball club) Präventionskonzept 2026](https://www.sc-potsdam.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Praeventionskonzept-zum-Kinderschutz-SC-Potsdam-e.V-1.pdf)
- [BK] Digital consent ages under Art. 8 GDPR: Spain 14 (LOPDGDD, LO 3/2018, art. 7), Germany 16 (no derogation in the BDSG), Poland 16 (no derogation). — [BOE LO 3/2018](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2018-16673) (not fetched; verify)

### Inferences
- The same product features meet the requirements in all four countries:
  - per-player image/publication consent records;
  - role scoping (no broad staff access to minors' data);
  - a documented position for the club's protection officer (delegado de protección, Polish standards coordinator, Italian Responsabile);
  - data-export/erasure tools.
- Storing staff criminal-record certificates is criminal-offence data (Art. 10 GDPR). The app should **not** store certificates. At most it should record a "verified on date X by Y" flag, if clubs ask for one.
- Age-dependent consent logic should be configurable per country (14 IT/ES, 16 DE/PL) if the app ever lets minors sign in themselves.

- DOSB-level obligations were not confirmed (the search found none).
- Polish art. 22c content on images and data is [BK] and needs verification.

## 4. Are player performance/physical-test data "health data" (Art. 9 GDPR)? DPA guidance on athlete data

### Takeaway
Skill ratings and match statistics are generally **not** health data. But the CJEU reads "data concerning health" broadly, including data from which health status can be **inferred** (Lindenapotheke C-21/23, Oct 2024; OT C-184/20). Physical-test results tied to body metrics, injuries, absences "due to injury", or disability can cross into Art. 9. No EDPB guidance specific to athlete data was found. CNIL (2024) gave guidance on athletes' disability data: collect only what is strictly necessary, consent in most cases, DPIA as good practice.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW via COMM] CJEU **Lindenapotheke (C-21/23, 4 Oct 2024)**: data entered when ordering non-prescription pharmacy medicines online is health data. The Court read "data concerning health" broadly, following OT (C-184/20), and treated data that lets health be inferred by deduction as special category data, whatever the controller's intent or the inference's accuracy. — [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/global/feeling-unwell-after-the-cjeus-lindenapotheke-decision); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/zh-hant/insights-and-events/insights/2024/10/ecj-lindenapotheke); [Matheson](https://www.matheson.com/insights/cjeu-publishes-a-flurry-of-gdpr-related-decisions/); [European Law Blog](https://www.europeanlawblog.eu/pub/y0sz1qv4)
- [DPA via COMM] **CNIL (May 2024)** on athletes' disability data: collect only if strictly necessary for a specific purpose (e.g. licence renewal), in most cases with the athlete's freely given consent. A DPIA is not always required but is good practice. — [A&O Shearman](https://aoshearman.com/insights/france-cnil-publishes-guidance-on-the-collection-of-data-relating-to-an-athletes-disability)
- [DPA] Dutch **AP**: argued that athlete consent cannot be a valid basis in anti-doping because it is not freely given (2017 position). The AP chair voiced concern about sports federations' commercial use of member data, and an investigation into tennis federation KNLTB was opened. — [AP position paper (openstate)](https://llms.openstate.eu/docs/64/49/64492c14-2d28-4e89-b40f-2ef5a73eb408.pdf); [NOS](https://nos.nl/l/2264001); [AVROTROS Radar](https://radar.avrotros.nl/artikel/onderzoek-naar-privacyschending-tennisbond-knltb-30371)
- [FED] NOC*NSF (Dutch Olympic Committee) guidance: health data covers any information on physical or mental health, not just medical treatment. A Dutch sports-association Q&A lists height, weight and health declarations as personal data. — [NOC*NSF handreiking](https://cdn.nocnsf.nl/media/nn1dr5gb/handreiking-delen-gegevens-delen-van-gegevens-binnen-het-topsport-begeleidingsteam.pdf); [Sport.nl AVG Q&A](https://clubbase.sport.nl/media/21900/questions-and-answers-avg-sportverenigingen-april-2018_v2.pdf)
- [COMM] Commentary: processing health data needs an Art. 9(2) condition on top of Art. 6. Clubs should be careful relying on explicit consent in imbalanced relationships. An academic thesis argues Art. 9(2)(h) may cover medical purposes but hardly "performance optimisation" alone, and notes the absence of CJEU case law. — [Penningtons](https://www.penningtonslaw.com/insights/gdpr-in-sport-trying-wearables-on-for-size/); [Irwin Mitchell](https://www.irwinmitchell.com/news-and-insights/expert-comment/post/102hi9n/data-protection-in-sport-tackling-gdpr-issues); [Lund thesis](https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/record/9226846/file/9235125.pdf)
- [FED] Swim Ireland's club guide: injury and physical/mental health data is sensitive and needs explicit consent. — [Swim Ireland GDPR Guide](https://swimireland.ie/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/02/GDPR-Guide.pdf)

### Inferences
- Classification for this app:
  - Skill ratings (1-10), development plans and scouting stats: ordinary personal data. They are still sensitive in practice because they concern minors and can affect selection.
  - Physical tests (sprint, jump, endurance): borderline. They describe physical capacity, and after Lindenapotheke, combined with body measurements or free-text notes (e.g. "knee pain", "asthma"), they may reveal health.
  - Injury/absence reasons, medical certificate status, height/weight/BMI: treat as Art. 9.
- Design implications:
  - Keep free-text "notes" fields out of the physical-test model, or warn users not to enter health information.
  - Don't store injury causes. If the club needs availability, use a neutral "unavailable" status.
  - If health fields are ever added, isolate them with stricter access (no viewers) and record explicit parental consent.
  - The club should run a DPIA. Minors plus systematic evaluation is a strong DPIA trigger on Garante/EDPB lists [BK].
- The Italian "certificato medico sportivo" (mandatory sports-medical certificate) is health data. Recording only "certificate valid until YYYY-MM-DD" is lower-risk but arguably still health-related (it reveals fitness). This is an inference; no source was found.

### Gaps
- No EDPB, Garante or AP guidance specific to amateur sports **performance** data (as opposed to health/disability/doping) was found.
- The CNIL original (French) was not fetched.

## 5. Sports data rights: who owns club-generated scouting stats? Database right, Football Dataco cases, Lega Volley/Genius exclusivity

### Takeaway
Raw match facts (who served, point outcomes) are not protected by copyright. The sui generis database right protects only a **substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting** data, not in **creating** it (Football Dataco v Yahoo C-604/10 for copyright; the fixtures/BHB line for sui generis). A club that scouts its own or a public match **creates** its own dataset and is its natural rightsholder. The Lega Volley–Genius Sports exclusivity (as published) covers **official live data and streams for betting**, not coaching data a club scouts itself. No league rule barring clubs from putting their own scouting data into third-party tools was found.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW via COMM] **Football Dataco v Yahoo (C-604/10, 1 Mar 2012)**: significant labour and skill in compiling a database cannot justify copyright under Directive 96/9 unless they express originality in the selection or arrangement of the data. Fixture lists failed. — [SCL Fixture List Protection](https://scl.org/?p=1955); [Law Gazette](https://lawgazette.co.uk/law/intellectual-property/65845.article)
- [LAW via COMM] Fixture lists had no sui generis database right because obtaining, verifying and presenting their contents involved no substantial investment separate from creating the data. — [SCL](https://scl.org/?p=1955); [Brittens judgment (EWHC 2010)](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2010/841)
- [LAW via COMM] **Football Dataco v Sportradar (C-173/11, 18 Oct 2012)**: concerned "Football Live", a database of live match data (goals, scorers, cards, fouls, substitutions) gathered by people at the matches. The CJEU ruled on where re-utilisation takes place: sending data from a server in one Member State to users in another can be re-utilisation in the targeted state. The UK court later accepted the live-data database could attract sui generis protection (investment in obtaining/verifying). — [SCL](https://www.scl.org/2595-database-right-ecj-judgment-in-football-data-co-v-sportradar/); [Publications Office](https://op.europa.eu/sk/publication-detail/-/publication/2dc4138a-4377-11e2-9b3b-01aa75ed71a1/language-en); [EWHC 2012/1185](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2012/1185)
- [COMM, primary page] **Lega Volley–Genius Sports (2021)**: Genius received exclusive worldwide rights to capture, manage and distribute **official live data and streams for betting purposes** for **Serie A2 and A3** men's leagues, plus automated GeniusLive production for LegaVolley.tv. Genius was already a technology partner (scoring, video review, coaching) to the federation and leagues. Duration not stated. Data Project and clubs' scouting data are not mentioned. — [Lega Volley announcement](https://www.legavolley.it/2021/exclusive-partnership-between-lega-volley-and-genius-sports-group/?lang=en); [Insider Sport](https://insidersport.com/2021/02/26/genius-expands-client-base-with-lega-volley-sign-up/)
- [COMM] SportBusiness reports a newer exclusive official data and streaming agreement between Genius and Lega Volley. Terms and date are paywalled and unverified. — [SportBusiness](https://media.sportbusiness.com/news/genius-serves-up-lega-volley-data-and-streaming-agreement/)
- [COMM] Genius Sports acquired Data Project (maker of DataVolley) on 18 Sept 2017. The App Store lists "Genius Sports Italy Srl" as developer of Data Volley 4 Web Client. — [Data Project announcement](https://www.dataproject.com/en/GeniusSportsAcquiresDataProject.html); [Data Project brand refresh](https://dataproject.com/en/DataProjectBrandRefresh); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id978033931)
- [BK] Directive 96/9/EC art. 7 gives the database maker (whoever takes the initiative and the risk of investing) the sui generis right against extraction or re-utilisation of a substantial part. CJEU *British Horseracing Board v William Hill* (C-203/02) excludes investment in **creating** data. — [EUR-Lex 31996L0009](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:31996L0009) (not fetched; verify)

### Inferences
- A club scouting its own matches, or public matches of opponents, with its own staff and its own app generates its own data. Facts about public play are free to record. Any database right in the resulting dataset (if the investment is substantial) belongs to the club as maker, not to the league or Genius. The vendor's terms should say the **club owns its match data** and the vendor processes it only on the club's behalf.
- Conflict with league exclusivity arises only if the app (or a club through it) **distributes live data for betting** or markets it as "official" league data. The product should prohibit and design against: public live-score feeds, public APIs, or resale of match data. It should keep scouting data private to the team, which also fits minors' data protection.
- Importing a .dvw that an **official league scorer** produced (e.g. files distributed by the league or exchanged between clubs) may carry contractual restrictions (league or exchange rules). The import UI should state that the user must have the right to use the file.

### Gaps
- No public Lega Volley or FIPAV regulation was found on clubs sharing scouting data with third-party tools or on .dvw file exchange between clubs (common practice in Italian volleyball, but no rule was located).
- The current (post-2021) Genius–Lega Volley deal terms and whether they extend to SuperLega or women's Lega Volley Femminile are unverified.

## 6. DataVolley .dvw format: openness, legality of parsing, EULA, file ownership, trademark naming

### Takeaway
.dvw is a proprietary but plain-text format. It is widely parsed by open-source tools (openvolley `datavolley` R package, which also reads VolleyStation .vsm, and files from Click&Scout). Under EU law, a **file format and a program's functionality are not protected by software copyright** (SAS Institute v WPL, C-406/10). Writing an independent parser from sample files and public knowledge is generally lawful, and Art. 6 of Directive 2009/24 additionally allows decompilation for interoperability within limits. The **Data Project EULA could not be found**, so contractual restrictions remain an open risk. Naming "DataVolley" to describe compatibility is permitted as descriptive or referential use under EUTMR art. 14 if it is done honestly. [Art. 6 and art. 14 content is BK.]

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Parsing**: build the importer from scratch, based on the structure seen in sample files and on the publicly available openvolley sources (check their licence; GPL-licensed code should not be copied into a proprietary app, though the knowledge of the format may be used). Do not decompile DataVolley and do not copy Data Project manuals. On that basis the importer is on solid legal ground under SAS v WPL.
- **Files belong to whoever scouted**: a .dvw created by a club's analyst is the club's data. The software licence binds the licensee's use of the *software*, not the factual content of match data. However, a EULA could contractually restrict the licensee (the club) from feeding outputs into competing tools. Such a term would bind the club, not the vendor, and its enforceability against the format/functionality principle is uncertain. Recommend: obtain and review the current DataVolley 4 licence before launch, and put a user warranty in the import flow ("I have the right to import this file").
- **Naming**: use "Import DataVolley (.dvw) files" or "compatible with .dvw files" in plain text. No Data Project logos, no "official"/"certified" claims. Add a disclaimer: "DataVolley is a trademark of its owner; this app is not affiliated with Data Project or Genius Sports."
- **Data protection on import**: a .dvw carries opponent players' names and jersey numbers (often minors). Importing creates processing of opponents' personal data by the club. This needs a legitimate-interest basis and minimisation (e.g. store opponents as numbers only, which matches the existing text-scouting approach of stripping names).
- Strategic risk: Genius Sports owns both DataVolley and Italian league data rights. Legal exposure is low, but commercial friction (format changes, encrypted or binary future formats, ToS changes) is possible. This is speculative.
