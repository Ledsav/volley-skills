# Pricing models, adoption and growth benchmarks for niche B2B sports software (as of Oct 2026)

Scope: inputs for (a) pricing model and price points and (b) a 3-5 season revenue forecast for a volleyball club app (scouting, player development, drill library, training planning), sold per season to clubs from Italian Serie B upward in Europe and the Americas, by a solo founder or very small team.

Note on source quality: many vendors do not publish prices. Where only aggregators or competitor blogs give figures, this is flagged. Older data is dated. "Estimate" marks figures that are not from a primary source.

## 1. How comparable sports software vendors price (unit, period, tiers, freemium, federation deals)

### Takeaway
The market splits into three bands. Cheap self-serve stat apps cost $5-$100 per team-season. Pro desktop scouting licences (Data Volley 4) cost about $299-$799 per year. Platform/video suites (Hudl) cost $500-$2,500 per team per year, with club-wide discounts of up to 50%. Per-team pricing with club bundles is the dominant unit. Annual or per-season terms are the norm, and pro/club tiers are usually "contact sales". Federation and league deals are a real channel (FIVB/Volleystation, Volleyball Alberta/Balltime).

USD, US-oriented. — [Hudl club volleyball pricing](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/club/volleyball)

### Inferences
- For a Serie B-and-up club buyer, the closest price anchors are Data Volley 4 (€299-€799/yr per licence, and most of these clubs already pay for it) and Hudl club (about $500-$1,600 per team per year). A tool covering development, drills and training planning, with scouting that complements DV4 rather than replacing it, plausibly sits at **€300-€1,200 per club per season** (estimate). Above roughly €1,500 it starts to compete with the whole Hudl or Volleystation budget.
- Per-team pricing with a club bundle (as Hudl does) suits a club with first team, B team and youth teams. A per-club flat fee with a team cap is simpler for a solo founder.
- The iStatVball "Team-Season, no auto-renewal" unit shows that per-season (not monthly) purchasing is accepted in volleyball. TeamBuildr's "annual = 10x monthly" is a common annual-prepay discount.
- A free tier or trial is common at the low end (Balltime, iStatVball, SoloStats). The pro end uses demos and quotes. A 14-30 day trial plus founding-customer pricing fits a solo founder better than a demo-only sales process.

### Gaps
- No public VolleyStation Pro price was found (quote only). Data Project euro list prices for the Italian or European storefront were not found, only USD and "from €299".
- No verified 2026 prices for Coach Logic, Pixellot, LongoMatch, Dartfish, InStat, or Hudl Assist for volleyball clubs.
- No public federation-wide pricing terms (for example, per-club rates under the FIVB/VolleyStation deal).

## 2. Small vertical B2B SaaS benchmarks: conversion, churn, sales cycle, seasonality, acquisition channels

### Takeaway
Secondary-source benchmarks put SMB annual logo churn at about 10-30% (Recurly's all-SaaS blended median is far lower at about 3%). Trial-to-paid is about 30-50% when a card is required up front, and only about 2-18% without a card or for freemium. No sports-specific sales-cycle data was found. In sports, buying works through reps and demos (Hudl), federation and association partnerships (Balltime, VolleyStation), and community word of mouth (Balltime).

### Cited Findings
- SMB annual logo churn: a 10-14% median is attributed to ChartMogul 2025 for the $1k-$10k ACV band (top quartile under 7%). Other secondary sources give 15-30%. One table shows 39% for the $500-$5k band, possibly derived from monthly data. These are secondary and conflicting. — [StealthAgents](https://stealthagents.com/research/saas-churn-rate-statistics-2026); [SaaSFlywheel](https://saasflywheel.io/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks-2026); [Growigami](https://growigami.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks)
- Recurly: median monthly churn for SaaS of 3.22% (2.16% voluntary, 1.06% involuntary). This is blended across B2B SaaS, not SMB-specific. The search summary called it "annual", but Recurly's benchmark is generally reported as monthly, so verify. — [Recurly churn benchmarks](https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/)
- Trial-to-paid (attributed to ChartMogul 2026 data, n=4,200, secondary): 34% median for card-required trials, 3.1% for trials without a card, 2.4% for freemium-to-paid. Another source gives 48.8% opt-out vs 18.2% opt-in (date and origin unclear). — [DigitalApplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/saas-marketing-statistics-2026-data-points-trends); [ChartMogul reports](https://chartmogul.com/reports/)
- Hudl club sales: account executives close by phone and online demo, and customer success managers own implementation and renewal revenue. Hudl's club team focuses on US and Canada. — [Hudl AE Club job](https://jobs.accel.com/companies/hudl/jobs/89299592-account-executive-club); [Hudl AE Club & Youth](https://zapply.jobs/jobs/8c683c79-9150-455b-8910-9f6769f3ed34/)
- Seat counts often get set during an optimistic budget cycle and are never revisited at renewal (third-party opinion). — [HoopBrief switching checklist](https://hoopbrief.com/blog/switching-from-hudl-checklist-2026)
- Balltime's growth channels: community engagement with players and coaches, Olympic and pro athlete advisors, social media, and the Hudl partnership. — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/balltime-breakdown)
- Balltime used regional associations and club networks as channels (Volleyball Alberta, Excelsior; Oct 2024). — [Volleyball Alberta](https://www.volleyballalberta.ca/balltime-x-volleyball-alberta/); [Excelsior](https://www.xlvball.org/2024/10/21/excelsior-empire-teams-up-with-balltime-to-bring-ai-powered-volleyball-analytics-to-more-clubs/)
- VolleyStation used FIVB/Volleyball World as an official-supplier channel. — [Volleyball World](https://en.volleyballworld.com/news/volleyball-world-and-fivb-join-forces-with-volleystation-to-revolutionize-volley)

### Inferences
- Forecast inputs (estimates, not sourced):
  - Annual logo churn: conservative 30%, base 20%, optimistic 12%. Small clubs churn when a coach leaves, relegation hits the budget, or a sponsor drops.
  - Trial-to-paid with a demo or onboarding call: about 20-35%.
  - Seasonality: European club budgets and staff are set in June-September, before season start in Aug-Oct, so expect most new logos and almost all renewals in a June-October window. NCAA fiscal years start July 1, and volleyball is a fall sport (Aug-Dec), so the US window is about April-August. Revenue for a per-season product will be lumpy: roughly 70-80% of bookings in one quarter (estimate).
- Sales cycle: with no data, assume 1-3 months from first contact for a small club where the head coach or director decides, and a full season (or a missed window) for clubs needing board sign-off (estimate).
- Channels most consistent with the evidence: coach-to-coach word of mouth, national federation or coach-association partnerships with member discounts (about 10%), coaching clinics, and coach influencers on social media. An official-supplier deal with a league is the main way to step-change growth.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025/2026 ChartMogul, KeyBanc or OpenView report figures were retrieved. All SMB benchmarks above are secondary.
- No sports-software-specific churn, conversion or sales-cycle data was found.
- No source documented actual buying windows for European clubs. The seasonality points above are inference.

## 3. Growth trajectories of comparable sports-tech companies

### Takeaway
Balltime (two founders, started 2022) reportedly reached about $100k MRR, 3,800+ teams by early 2025 and 12,000+ teams at its Feb 2025 sale to Hudl for an undisclosed "8-figure" sum. That is a best-case path built on freemium AI video for youth and club volleyball, not on pro clubs. Category leaders (Hudl, Veo, Spond) are at tens of thousands of clubs or teams after many years and heavy funding. VolleyStation's only public financial signal is 60.5% sales growth in 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Balltime: founded 2022 by Dan Banon (CEO) and Tom Raz (CTO). It took about 6 months to build the prototype, has about 12 employees (estimate), was profitable, and is reported at about $100k/month revenue (about $1.2M/yr). It was acquired by Hudl in 2025 for an undisclosed "8-figure" sum. Starter Story figures are self-reported or estimated (article dated 28 Feb 2025). — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/balltime-breakdown)
- At acquisition (6 Feb 2025), Hudl said Balltime served more than 12,000 indoor and beach volleyball teams and more than 125,000 athletes. An older figure is 3,800+ teams. — [Hudl blog](https://www.hudl.com/blog/hudl-expands-volleyball-focus-through-game-changing-acquisition-of-balltime); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250206384625/en); [Profluence podcast](https://profluence.com/podcast/dan-banon-hudl-balltime-acquisition/)
- Balltime marketed itself as "trusted by 350+ youth clubs". — [Hudl Balltime pricing page](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/balltime) (as summarized in search)
- Hudl's volleyball suite serves more than 40,000 high schools, clubs, colleges and national teams (Feb 2025 release). — [Hudl blog](https://www.hudl.com/blog/hudl-expands-volleyball-focus-through-game-changing-acquisition-of-balltime)
- Hudl was founded in 2006 in Lincoln, Nebraska. The Nebraska Huskers football team was the first paying customer. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudl); [Omaha Magazine](https://www.omahamagazine.com/?p=36012)
- Hudl revenue (Getlatka estimates): $45.9M in 2021, $69.4M in 2023. The team base is reported as 230,000+ (Dealroom) or 325,000+ (Multiples.vc). — [Getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/hudl); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/hudl/); [Multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/hudl)

### Inferences
- The addressable pro and semi-pro market is small, so Balltime's numbers do not transfer. Clubs from Serie B upward in Europe plus NCAA and pro programs in the Americas number in the low thousands, not tens of thousands. A solo-founder forecast in the tens to low hundreds of clubs by season 3-5 is more realistic (estimate). Example scenarios at about €600 ARPA:
  - Conservative: 10 / 25 / 40 clubs (seasons 1-3).
  - Base: 20 / 50 / 90.
  - Optimistic: 40 / 100 / 180, needing a federation or league partnership.
- Spond and Veo show that freemium or hardware plus subscription can reach scale in sports, but only with VC funding.

- VolleyStation's absolute revenue, funding and club count are not public.
- Balltime's revenue comes from a single, partly estimated source.

## 4. Price sensitivity and discounting evidence

### Takeaway
Direct statements from semi-pro clubs about software budgets were not found. The observable discount patterns are club-wide bundles (Hudl, up to 50%), multi-team volume pricing (iStatVball), association member discounts (about 10%, Balltime), and annual-prepay discounts of about two months free (TeamBuildr).

### Cited Findings
- Hudl gives up to 50% off for multi-team and club-wide packages, and "additional discounts" for club volleyball. — [Hudl club soccer](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/club/soccer); [Hudl club volleyball](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/club/volleyball)
- iStatVball's price per team-season falls from $39.99 for 1 team to about $25 each for 4 teams ($99.99). — [SmashVision](https://smashvision.ai/blog/how-much-does-volleyball-stats-software-cost)
- Balltime gave Volleyball Alberta members a 10% discount. — [Volleyball Alberta](https://www.volleyballalberta.ca/balltime-x-volleyball-alberta/)
- TeamBuildr's annual price equals 10x the monthly rate. — [TeamBuildr pricing](https://www.teambuildr.com/pricing)
- In semi-pro football, the Veo + Nacsport combination is chosen for its "balance between cost and functionality". Wyscout and StatsBomb are considered "very high cost". — [FSI](https://fsi.training/en/blogs/best-football-analytics-tools-2026)
- SmashVision's own blog warns that euro and dollar prices are not directly comparable and that clubs should check whether existing Hudl plans already cover AI. This points to budget overlap as a sales objection. — [SmashVision best apps](https://smashvision.ai/blog/best-volleyball-stats-apps)

### Inferences
- Suggested levers (estimates):
  - Founding-customer discount of 30-50% locked for 2-3 seasons.
  - Multi-season prepay of about 15-20% off.
  - Federation or association member discount of about 10-20%.
  - Club bundle at about 40-50% off the per-team list price.
- Existing tool spend (DV4, VolleyStation, Hudl) is the main objection, so position the product as complementary to the scouting software clubs already run.

### Gaps
- No survey or interview data on what Serie B / A2 / PlusLiga / NCAA programs say they spend or would pay for training and development software.

## 5. Costs to serve: hosting, LLM, payments and VAT, GDPR, COPPA, FERPA

### Takeaway
Infrastructure and AI costs per club are negligible next to the price (well under €5-€10 per club per season, estimate). The material costs are payment and tax handling (about 5-8% with a merchant of record) and compliance work: a GDPR processor DPA, minors' consent ages of 13-16 depending on the country, the amended COPPA rule for under-13s, and FERPA school-official contract terms for US colleges.

### Cited Findings
**Hosting (Firebase/Firestore)**
- Firestore free quota: 50,000 reads and 20,000 writes per day. Overage is about $0.03 per 100k reads and $0.09 per 100k writes (single-region rates). Other 2026 guides quote $0.06 and $0.18, probably multi-region. — [Google Cloud Firestore pricing](https://cloud.google.com/firestore/pricing); [BudgetForge](https://www.budgetforge.dev/tools/firebase-pricing-2026)

**LLM API**
- Groq Llama 3.1 8B: $0.05 input / $0.08 output per million tokens. Groq Llama 3.3 70B: $0.59 / $0.79. Batch is reportedly about 50% cheaper. — [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/groq-pricing/); [Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/comparisons/fireworks-vs-groq)
- OpenAI gpt-4.1-mini: $0.40 input / $1.60 output per million tokens (verified against OpenAI on 2026-10-16 per the source). Batch is $0.20 / $0.80. — [PricePerToken](https://pricepertoken.com/pricing-page/model/openai-gpt-4.1-mini); [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-4.1-mini)

**Payments and VAT**
- Paddle (merchant of record) charges 5% + $0.50 per transaction, covering processing, tax registration and remittance, fraud and billing. Currency conversion can add about 2-3%. — [CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/compare/stripe-vs-paddle/); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/paddle-vs-stripe-vs-merchant-of-record-2026)
- Stripe charges 2.9% + $0.30 for cards, plus 0.5% for Stripe Tax and extra for Billing. The all-in cost is about 4-7%. One source claims Stripe Tax does not automate OSS filing (not verified with Stripe). One source estimates $5k-$15k per year in accounting costs for self-handled EU and US sales tax. Paddle is said to make most sense below about $8-10k MRR. — [Cut-the-SaaS](https://cut-the-saas.com/guides/stripe-vs-paddle); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/paddle-vs-stripe-vs-merchant-of-record-2026)
- Above €10,000 per year in EU cross-border digital sales, the seller must charge VAT at the customer's member-state rate (via OSS). This is secondary; check the EU source. — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/paddle-vs-stripe-vs-merchant-of-record-2026)

**GDPR (minors)**
- GDPR Art. 8 digital-consent age: the default is 16, and member states may lower it to no less than 13. Italy is 14, Spain 14, Germany 16, Poland 16. Austria, Bulgaria, Cyprus and Lithuania are also 14. Sources are secondary. — [Bird & Bird Children in the Digital World](https://childreninthedigitalworld.twobirds.com/home/eu); [Presencis Art. 8](https://presencis.com/regulations/gdpr/article-8/)

**COPPA (US, under 13)**
- The amended COPPA Rule was published 22 Apr 2025 and took effect 23 Jun 2025, with full compliance due 22 Apr 2026, now enforced. It requires separate verifiable parental consent for disclosure to third parties (for example, targeted ads), a written data-retention policy, and covers an expanded definition of personal information. — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/unpacking-ftcs-coppa-amendments-what-you-need-know)

**FERPA (US colleges)**
- A vendor can receive education records under FERPA's school-official exception if it meets four conditions. It must perform an institutional service, be under the institution's direct control, meet the school's annual-notice criteria, and use the data only for authorized purposes with no re-disclosure. Contracts usually add security standards and bans on targeted ads and profiling. — [ByteBack Law](https://www.bytebacklaw.com/2017/02/5-simple-rules-for-ferpa-contracting-compliance/) (2017); [CDT](https://cdt.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/UPDATED-2021-01-21-Student-Privacy-2pg-Commercial-Edtech-Final.pdf) (2021)

### Inferences
- Firestore cost per club is small. Assume a club generates about 20-50k reads per training or match day across staff, multiplied by about 200 active days per season, which is about 4-10M reads, or about $1-$6 per season plus writes (estimate). A few hundred clubs stay in the low hundreds of $ per month.
- LLM cost per club is small. Assume text scouting plus drafting uses about 2M tokens per club per season. On gpt-4.1-mini that is about $1-$3 per club per season, and on Groq 8B about $0.20 (estimate). Keep a per-club token cap.
- Payments: at about €600 per club per season, Paddle takes about €30-€35 per sale (about 5-6%) and removes VAT and OSS filing work. For a solo founder below about $10k MRR, a merchant of record is the cheaper option once accounting time is counted.
- Compliance:
  - In the EU, the app is a processor and the club is the controller. A GDPR Art. 28 DPA, a sub-processor list (Google Cloud/Firebase, the LLM provider, Paddle) and EU data residency are expected sales requirements. Name-stripping before the LLM, as `textScout.ts` already does, helps.
  - In the US, NCAA athletes are mostly 18+ (COPPA is largely irrelevant), but colleges will require FERPA school-official contract language. US clubs with under-13 players trigger COPPA.

- No US state privacy laws (for example, student-athlete NIL or biometric data) were checked.

## 6. Localization: which languages matter and how much

### Takeaway
No source quantified localization's effect on adoption in sports software. Market structure points to Italian, Polish, Turkish and Portuguese (Brazil) as the priority non-English languages for pro volleyball buyers, with Spanish, French and German second (inference).
