# Data Processing Agreement

> **Draft — under legal review.**

Version 2026-10-22.

This Data Processing Agreement (the "DPA") sets out how the operator processes personal data for the club when the club uses Volley Skills (the "service"). It is part of the contract formed by the Terms of Service (the "Terms") and is accepted together with them. Words defined in the Terms have the same meaning here.

**The controller.** The club named at sign-up (the "club"). The club decides why and how the personal data it puts into the service is processed.

**The processor.** {{OPERATOR_NAME}}, a natural person (an individual, not a company), residing at {{OPERATOR_ADDRESS}}, {{OPERATOR_COUNTRY}} (the "operator").{{OPERATOR_REGISTRATION}}{{OPERATOR_VAT}}

This DPA follows Article 28(3) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the "GDPR") point by point. Annex I describes the processing, Annex II the security measures and Annex III the sub-processors. The clauses of the Terms on assignment (T14), law and courts (T21), language (T22) and notices (T23) also apply to this DPA.

## D1. Subject matter, duration, nature and purpose

The operator processes personal data on the club's behalf only to provide the service to the club: hosting the club's data and running the features the club's people use.

The processing lasts for the term of the Terms, plus the notice, transition and retrieval periods of the exit rules in T10 of the Terms, plus the time needed for deletion under D8.

Annex I describes the nature and purpose of the processing, the types of personal data and the categories of data subjects.

## D2. Documented instructions

The operator processes the club's personal data only on the club's documented instructions. These instructions are:

- the Terms and this DPA;
- the club's use of the service's features, including the settings club admins choose (for example, switching AI features or growth tests on or off);
- any further written instructions from a club admin that the operator agrees are within the service.

The operator may also process the data where the law of the European Union or of a Member State requires it. In that case, the operator tells the club before processing, unless that law forbids telling it.

If the operator considers that an instruction infringes the GDPR or other data protection law, it tells the club without delay and need not follow that instruction unless the club changes it so that it complies.

## D3. Confidentiality and operator access

The operator is a single person and has no employees. The operator alone holds administrative access to the infrastructure that runs the service. If anyone else is later given access to club data, the operator makes sure that, before they get it, they are bound by a duty of confidentiality.

The operator has no access to club data inside the service. The service's security rules refuse the operator's platform account any read of a club's members, teams, players, matches, library, guides and section access, and automated tests check this. The operator's platform account sees only the club's profile and its contract records: its status, its acceptances of the Terms and this DPA, and its status log.

The one exception is a club that itself adds the operator's personal account as one of its members. That access is the club's own grant, under the club's control like any other member's, and the operator then acts as a member of that club, not as the operator.

At the infrastructure level, the operator can technically read club data. The operator reads a club's data only:

- to run the service, as automated processing;
- on the club's documented request, such as a support case or a restore;
- when the law requires it.

The operator notes each such access in the club's status log, with the date, the reason and who asked, unless it is part of the service's routine automated processing (such as the weekly backup, or the deletion of an ended club's data after its retrieval period).

Backups are opened only to check that they open, or to restore. A restore of production data into the operator's local test environment is stopped as soon as the work is done.

## D4. Security

The operator takes the technical and organisational measures described in Annex II, which take into account the state of the art, the cost of implementation, the nature of the processing and the risks to the people concerned, as Article 32 of the GDPR requires.

The operator may change these measures over time, but not in a way that lowers the overall level of protection described in Annex II.

## D5. Sub-processors

The club gives the operator a general written authorisation to engage the sub-processors on the sub-processor list (Annex III). The list in force is published at /legal/subprocessors and is versioned separately from this DPA.

Before adding or replacing a sub-processor, the operator gives the club 30 days' notice, by a banner in the service shown to club admins and by an email the operator sends to the club's owner email.

The club may object within 15 days of the notice, on reasonable grounds relating to data protection, by writing to {{OPERATOR_CONTACT_EMAIL}}. The operator and the club then try in good faith to resolve the objection. If they cannot, the club's sole remedy is to end the contract before the change takes effect, with a pro-rata refund of the fees for the rest of the season.

The operator imposes on each sub-processor, by contract, data protection obligations that give the same protection as this DPA, as Article 28(4) of the GDPR requires. The operator remains responsible to the club for its sub-processors' performance of those obligations.

## D6. Assistance with data subjects' rights

The service gives the club the tools to answer requests from data subjects itself: team admins and club admins can view, correct and delete player records, and club admins can delete teams and export all the club's data.

If a data subject sends a request about club data to the operator, the operator passes it to the club without undue delay and does not answer it itself, unless the club instructs it to.

Where the club needs help beyond these tools, the operator assists at its published hourly rate of {{SUPPORT_HOURLY_RATE}} per hour, agreed in advance with the club.

## D7. Assistance with security, breaches and impact assessments

The operator helps the club meet its obligations under Articles 32 to 36 of the GDPR, taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to the operator.

**Personal data breaches.** The operator notifies the club of a personal data breach affecting the club's data without undue delay and, where feasible, within 48 hours of becoming aware of it. The notice goes to the club's owner email. It describes, as far as then known, the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed. Information not yet available is given as soon as it is. This leaves the club time to notify its supervisory authority within the 72 hours the GDPR allows.

**Impact assessments and consultations.** The operator provides the DPIA support pack, published at /legal/dpia-pack, to help the club carry out its own data protection impact assessment and any prior consultation with a supervisory authority. Further help is charged as in D6.

## D8. End of processing: return and deletion

When the contract ends, the club chooses what happens to its data:

- **Return.** Club admins export the club's data through the free, self-service export during the retrieval period of at least 30 days set out in T10 of the Terms.
- **Deletion.** Within 30 days after the retrieval period ends, the operator deletes the club's data from the service, whether or not the club exported it.

The operator's encrypted backups are deleted within 35 days of being taken. Copies of the club's data in backups therefore expire within 35 days after the end of processing. The operator uses no Google-managed backups.

After deletion, the operator keeps a record of the contract as its own evidence, as a controller: the club's name, country, owner email, the relevant dates, the club's acceptances of the Terms and this DPA, and the club's status log. This record holds no player data.

The operator keeps no other copy of the club's data, unless the law of the European Union or of a Member State requires it to.

## D9. Information and audits

The operator makes available to the club the information needed to show that it meets its obligations under Article 28 of the GDPR. Audits are met first by documentation:

- this DPA and its Annex II;
- a summary of the automated security-rules test suite that enforces the isolation of club data;
- the certifications and audit reports that Google publishes for the services on the sub-processor list;
- written answers to a reasonable questionnaire from the club, given within 30 days.

An on-site or remote audit by the club, or by an independent auditor bound by confidentiality whom the club appoints, takes place only:

- if a supervisory authority requires it; or
- after a confirmed personal data breach affecting the club's data.

Such an audit is at the club's cost, needs 30 days' written notice, and may take place at most once a year, unless a supervisory authority requires more. It must not give access to other clubs' data.

## D10. Transfers outside the European Union

The club's data is stored in Cloud Firestore in the European Union (the EU multi-region location eur3).

Some Google services the service relies on may process personal data outside the European Union, in particular in the United States: Firebase Authentication (sign-in), Firebase Hosting (delivery of the application's files) and reCAPTCHA (used by App Check against abuse). These transfers rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, under which Google is certified, and on the standard contractual clauses in Google's data processing terms.

On the date of this version, the EU-US Data Privacy Framework remains valid: the General Court upheld it on 3 September 2025 (Latombe), and an appeal is pending before the Court of Justice (C-703/25 P). If it is invalidated, the operator relies on the standard contractual clauses and tells the club of any change this requires.

The operator does not transfer the club's data outside the European Union in any other way.

## D12. The club's obligations

As controller, the club:

- has a lawful basis for every personal data record it puts into the service;
- informs players and guardians about the processing and obtains guardians' consent where the law requires it;
- records a guardian's consent in the service before using a photo of a minor in an award card; the service enables photos of a minor only once that consent is recorded. For photos of adults, the club relies on its own lawful basis;
- puts no special categories of personal data (such as health data) into the service, with one exception under its control: if the club switches growth tests on, height and body mass are processed and may be health data, and the club is responsible for the condition under Article 9 of the GDPR that allows it (typically a guardian's explicit consent) and for covering it in its impact assessment;
- decides whether to switch AI features on; if it does, the AI provider its staff choose, with their own key, is the club's own processor, and the club is responsible for its agreement with that provider;
- carries out its own data protection impact assessment where one is required. Lists published by supervisory authorities can include processing of minors' data and the use of new technologies such as AI;
- keeps its members and their access up to date, and removes people who should no longer have access;
- names a contact for the operator: the club's owner email, which it keeps current.

## Annex I. Description of the processing

**Data subjects.**

- Players, many of whom are minors.
- Players' parents or guardians.
- The club's coaches, staff and other members who use the service.
- Opposing players, identified by jersey number only.

**Personal data.**

- Identity and contact data of players: name, date of birth, nationality, licence number, jersey number, playing position, shirt size, phone, team, and the consents recorded for them (including guardian consent and photo consent).
- Identity and contact data of guardians: name, relation to the player, phone and email.
- Data about staff and members: email, role in the club, team and section access, and records of who created or changed data and when.
- Sports performance data: skill ratings on a 1–10 scale with notes, development plans, physical tests (such as jumps, sprints, reaction and strength), match line-ups, scouting events and statistics, training plans and calendars, and award choices.

**Special categories.** None are intended, with one exception the club controls: if the club switches growth tests on, height and body mass are processed and may be health data (Article 9 of the GDPR). Growth tests are off by default.

**Nature of the processing.** Storage, organisation, retrieval, display, computation of derived values (such as average scores, levels and match statistics), export and deletion, carried out continuously while the service is used.

**Purpose.** Providing the service to the club: managing teams, player profiles and development, physical testing, a training library and calendars, and match scouting and reports.

**Duration and retention.** The term of the Terms, plus the exit periods in T10 of the Terms, plus deletion under D8. During the term, the club decides how long it keeps each record and deletes records in the service.

## Annex II. Technical and organisational measures

**Isolation and access control**

- Every read and write of club data made through the service is checked by the service's security rules, which confine each person to the clubs they are a member of. An automated test suite checks these rules, including tests that one club cannot reach another club's data.
- The operator's maintenance scripts (backup, restore, deletion of an ended club's data) run with administrative credentials outside these rules, under the operator access policy in D3.
- The operator's platform account is refused access to club data by the same rules, and automated tests check this (see D3).
- Access inside a club is role-based: club admins, members, team admins per team, access to a single player's record for the members a team admin names, and section access for the exercise library, trainings and guides.
- Sign-in uses Firebase Authentication with passwordless email links or Google sign-in. The service stores no passwords.
- App Check with reCAPTCHA v3 checks that requests come from the service's own web app, to protect it from abuse.

**Encryption and location**

- All traffic is encrypted in transit with TLS.
- Google encrypts the stored data at rest.
- Club data is stored in Cloud Firestore in the EU multi-region location eur3.

**Data minimisation by design**

- The data model holds only what the service's features need. Apart from the optional growth tests below, it has no field that asks for health or medical data.
- Growth tests (height, body mass) are off by default. While they are off, the security rules refuse new growth-test records.
- AI features are off by default for each club, and only a club admin can switch them on. They use a provider and key chosen by the club's staff; the service stores the key only in that staff member's browser.
- In text scouting, the service replaces the club's player names with jersey numbers before any text is sent to the AI provider. AI drafting sends only the coach's request and the text of exercises in the club's library, and no player data from the service.
- Photos for award cards are processed on the device only and are never uploaded or stored by the service. Photos of minors can be used only once a guardian's photo consent is recorded in the service.
- Signing out removes the AI key and unsent text-scouting notes from the device and clears the service's offline copy of club data (if another open tab of the service still uses that copy, it is cleared at a later sign-out).

**Export and deletion**

- Club admins can export all the club's data at any time as a ZIP file, with a README that lists the data and its formats.
- Team admins and club admins can delete players, and club admins can delete teams, in the service. Deleting a player also deletes their physical tests.
- After the retrieval period, the operator deletes the club's data with a purge procedure that leaves only the contract record described in D8.

**Backups**

- The operator takes a backup of the service's database every week.
- Each backup is encrypted with AES-256-GCM, with a key sealed with the operator's own key, so only the operator can open it.
- Backups are held only on the operator's encrypted devices in the European Union, and no cloud storage provider holds them.
- Each backup is deleted within 35 days of being taken.
- The operator's scripts log counts only, never personal data.

**Organisation**

- The operator is a single person with no employees; anyone given access later is first bound by confidentiality (D3).
- The operator follows the operator access policy in D3: club data is read only to run the service, on the club's documented request, or when the law requires it, and each such access outside routine automated processing is noted in the club's status log.
- Incident response: when the operator becomes aware of a possible personal data breach, it contains it, assesses the risk, notifies the affected clubs as set out in D7, and keeps a written record of the breach and the measures taken.
