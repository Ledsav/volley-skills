# DPIA support pack

> **Draft — under legal review.**

Version 2026-10-22.

This pack helps a club carry out its own data protection impact assessment (DPIA) for its use of Volley Skills (the "service"). It is provided by {{OPERATOR_NAME}} (the "operator"), the club's processor, under D7 of the Data Processing Agreement (the "DPA").

**This pack is not a DPIA.** The club is the controller: it decides whether a DPIA is required, carries it out, and records its own conclusions. The pack describes the service and the measures built into it, so the club can rely on those facts, and ends with a checklist for the club's own assessment.

## 1. When a DPIA may be required

Under Article 35 of the GDPR, a DPIA is required when processing is likely to result in a high risk to people's rights and freedoms. Supervisory authorities publish lists of processing that needs one. Those lists can include the systematic processing of data about vulnerable people such as minors, the evaluation of people's performance, and the use of new technologies such as AI. A club that records minors' data season after season, and especially one that switches growth tests or AI features on, should check its own supervisory authority's list and expect a DPIA to be needed.

## 2. Description of the processing

Annex I of the DPA describes the processing in full. In short:

- **Data subjects:** players (many of them minors), their parents or guardians, the club's coaches, staff and other members, and opposing players (by jersey number only).
- **Data:** players' identity and contact details, guardians' contact details, staff emails and access, and sports performance data (skill ratings, development plans, physical tests, match line-ups, scouting statistics, trainings and award choices).
- **Special categories:** none intended. If the club switches growth tests on, height and body mass are recorded and may be health data.
- **Purpose:** managing teams, player development, physical testing, trainings and match scouting.
- **Who has access:** the club's own members, according to the roles and access club admins give them (club admin, member, team admin per team, section access for the exercise library, trainings and guides). The operator has no access to club data inside the service (DPA D3).
- **Retention:** the club decides how long it keeps each record and deletes records in the service. When the contract ends, the club can export everything and the data is then deleted (DPA D8).

## 3. Data flows

- **Browser to database.** Staff use the service in a web browser. The browser reads and writes club data directly in Cloud Firestore, stored in the European Union. Every request is checked by the service's security rules.
- **Sign-in.** Sign-in goes through Firebase Authentication, with an email link or with Google.
- **Abuse protection.** App Check uses Google's reCAPTCHA, which assesses device and browser signals.
- **Offline copy.** The browser keeps an offline copy of the club data in use, so matches can be scouted without a signal. Signing out clears it (if another open tab of the service still uses it, at a later sign-out).
- **AI (optional).** If the club switches AI on, the browser sends text directly to the AI provider chosen by the club's staff, with their own key. The operator does not receive this text.
- **Photos.** Photos for award cards are processed in the browser only and are never uploaded or stored.
- **Files made on the device.** The club data export (a ZIP file) and training PDFs are built in the browser and saved where the staff member chooses.
- **Backups.** The operator takes weekly encrypted backups of the database, kept on the operator's encrypted devices in the European Union and deleted within 35 days.

The sub-processors and their locations are listed at /legal/subprocessors.

## 4. Typical risks and the measures built in

### Minors' data

**Risk.** Players are often minors. Their data deserves specific protection, and unauthorised access or disclosure affects children.

**Built in.**

- Each club's data is isolated by the security rules, and an automated test suite checks that one club cannot reach another's data.
- Inside the club, access is role-based, so staff see only what club admins grant them.
- Guardian consent can be recorded per player.
- The data model asks for no health or medical data, apart from the optional growth tests.

**For the club to decide.** The lawful basis for each kind of record, the information given to guardians, and who in the club gets access.

### Skill ratings and profiling

**Risk.** Skill ratings (1–10), levels, development plans and match statistics evaluate players' performance. This can amount to profiling under Article 4(4) of the GDPR, and ratings can affect how players are treated, for example in team selection.

**Built in.**

- Coaches enter the ratings. The service computes averages and levels from them, and can show match-data suggestions next to skill scores, but it makes no decisions about players.
- AI is never used to rate, rank or select players.
- Ratings are visible only to the club members given access to the team.

**For the club to decide.** How ratings are used, that no decision with significant effects on a player is taken solely by automated means, and how players and guardians are told about the ratings and can ask about them.

### AI drafting and text scouting

**Risk.** Text sent to an AI provider may contain personal data, and the provider may be outside the European Union.

**Built in.**

- AI features are off by default, and only a club admin can switch them on for the club.
- In text scouting, the service replaces the club's player names with jersey numbers before sending text to the AI provider.
- AI drafting of exercises and trainings sends only the coach's request and the text of exercises in the club's library, with no player data from the service.
- AI output is a draft that the coach reviews.
- The AI key is kept only in the staff member's browser and is removed on sign-out.

**For the club to decide.** Whether to switch AI on, which providers its staff may use (the provider is the club's own processor, so the club needs an agreement with it and must check where it processes data), and instructions to staff not to type names, health details or other personal data into AI requests.

### Photos

**Risk.** Images of minors are sensitive, and award cards are made to be shared.

**Built in.**

- Photos and cut-outs are processed on the device only and are never uploaded or stored by the service.
- A photo of a minor can be used only once a guardian's photo consent is recorded in the service.
- The service warns staff before sharing images of minors.

**For the club to decide.** How it collects guardians' photo consent, its own lawful basis for photos of adults, and where staff may share award cards.

### Growth tests and health data

**Risk.** Height and body mass of minors may be health data under Article 9 of the GDPR.

**Built in.** Growth tests are off by default. While they are off, they are hidden and the security rules refuse new growth-test records. Existing growth data stays readable.

**For the club to decide.** Whether to switch growth tests on and, if so, the Article 9 condition it relies on (typically a guardian's explicit consent), and how this DPIA covers it.

### Free-text notes

**Risk.** Notes fields (on skills, development plans, physical tests, trainings and matches) could be used to record health, injury or other sensitive information.

**Built in.** The Terms forbid recording health or medical data outside growth tests (T7).

**For the club to decide.** Instructions to staff on what notes may contain.

### Unauthorised access and shared devices

**Risk.** A lost or shared device, or a person who keeps access after leaving the club.

**Built in.**

- Sign-in uses email links or Google, with no passwords stored by the service.
- Signing out removes the AI key and AI drafts from the device and clears the offline copy (if another open tab of the service still uses it, at a later sign-out).
- Club admins add and remove members and their access in the club area.

**For the club to decide.** A routine to remove leavers' access, and a rule that staff sign out on shared devices.

### Loss of data and the end of the contract

**Risk.** Data lost by mistake, or the club's data held longer than needed.

**Built in.**

- Weekly encrypted backups, deleted within 35 days.
- A free export of all the club's data, with a README listing its contents and formats.
- Deletion of the club's data after the retrieval period (DPA D8).

**For the club to decide.** Its retention periods for players who leave, and safe storage of any export or PDF it downloads, since those files are outside the service.

### Transfers outside the European Union

**Risk.** Data processed outside the European Union.

**Built in.** Club data is stored in the European Union. The Google services that may process data in the United States rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses (DPA D10).

**For the club to decide.** The transfers made by any AI provider its staff use.

## 5. Checklist for the club's own assessment

Work through each point of this checklist and keep the answers with the club's DPIA records.

- We have checked our supervisory authority's list and decided whether a DPIA is required, and recorded why.
- We have identified the lawful basis for each kind of record (player details, guardian contacts, ratings, physical tests, match data).
- We have told players and guardians, in clear language suited to minors, what we record, why, who sees it and how long we keep it.
- We record guardian consent in the service where we rely on it, including photo consent for minors before any photo is used in an award card.
- We have decided how skill ratings are used, and that no decision with significant effects on a player is taken solely by automated means.
- We have decided whether to switch growth tests on and, if we have, recorded our Article 9 condition.
- We have decided whether to switch AI features on and, if we have, which providers staff may use, under what agreement, and where they process data.
- We have told staff not to record health, injury or other sensitive information in notes, and not to type personal data into AI requests.
- We give each person only the access they need, and remove access when someone leaves the club.
- Staff sign out on shared devices.
- We have set retention periods, and delete players' records when they are no longer needed.
- We know how to export our data and where we keep any exports and PDFs safely.
- We have a contact person for data protection questions and keep the club's owner email current.
- We know how to handle a data subject's request, and how the operator will notify us of a breach (DPA D7).
- We review this assessment at least once a season, and when we switch a feature on.
